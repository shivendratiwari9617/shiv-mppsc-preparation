# Jigs & Fixtures — Design Basics (जिग व फिक्सचर डिजाइन के मूल सिद्धांत)

## Jigs & Fixtures — Basic Purpose

Work-holding devices used in **mass/batch production** to hold, support, and **locate** a workpiece precisely and repeatably during a machining operation — eliminating the need for repeated manual marking-out/measuring/alignment for each identical part, which greatly improves production **speed, consistency, and interchangeability** of parts.

## Jig vs Fixture — the Critical Distinction (extremely high-yield)

| | Jig | Fixture |
|---|---|---|
| Primary function | Holds AND **guides the cutting tool** directly (via built-in bushings/guides) | Holds and **locates** the workpiece only — does **NOT** guide the tool |
| Typical operation | **Drilling, reaming, tapping** (tool-guiding operations, where the tool path is a simple straight line through a bushing) | **Milling, turning, grinding, welding, inspection** (operations where the tool path is complex/generated by the machine itself, not simply guided through a fixed bushing) |
| Attachment to machine | Usually **not** clamped/bolted to the machine table (often light enough to be moved by hand, or the drill itself moves through it) | Usually **clamped/bolted rigidly** to the machine table, since cutting forces are typically higher and the setup must resist them without shifting |
| Weight | Generally **lighter** | Generally **heavier/more robust** |

**Simple memory rule: "Jig guides the tool; Fixture holds/locates the work (and is itself fixed to the machine)."** — this single distinguishing rule resolves nearly every question on this topic.

## Locating Principles

### The 3-2-1 Principle (6-Point Location Principle) — foundational concept
A rigid body in 3D space has **6 degrees of freedom** (3 translational along X/Y/Z, 3 rotational about X/Y/Z). To fully and uniquely locate a workpiece (restrain all 6 degrees of freedom), the standard practice uses:
- **3 locating points on the primary (largest) reference surface** — restrains 3 degrees of freedom (movement along one axis + rotation about the other two).
- **2 locating points on a secondary surface** — restrains 2 more degrees of freedom.
- **1 locating point on a tertiary surface** — restrains the final degree of freedom.

**Total: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 locating points, restraining all 6 degrees of freedom** — this is the origin of the widely-used term "**3-2-1 principle**" in jig/fixture design, a very frequently tested specific fact.

## Key Jig/Fixture Elements

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| **Locators** | Establish/define the precise, repeatable position of the workpiece relative to the jig/fixture (and hence relative to the tool/machine) |
| **Clamps** | Hold the workpiece **firmly** against the locators, resisting the cutting forces during the operation, without disturbing the established location |
| **Bushings (Bushes)** | Hardened, wear-resistant guide bushings in a **jig**, through which the cutting tool (drill, reamer) passes, to guide it precisely to the correct position |
| **Jig Body/Fixture Body** | The main structural frame holding all the above elements together, and (for a fixture) providing the means of clamping to the machine table |

## Types of Bushings (Drill Jig Specific)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Fixed (Press-Fit) Bushing** | Permanently pressed into the jig body — used where only one operation (e.g., only drilling) is needed at that location |
| **Renewable (Slip/Fixed-Renewable) Bushing** | Can be removed and replaced when worn, without disturbing the jig body itself |
| **Liner Bushing** | A separate, permanently installed outer sleeve, into which a renewable bushing is fitted — protects the jig body itself from wear when bushings need frequent replacement |

## Advantages of Using Jigs & Fixtures

- Ensures **interchangeability**/consistency of parts (critical for mass production and assembly-line compatibility).
- **Reduces production time** (eliminates repeated marking-out, measuring, and alignment for each part).
- Allows use of **less-skilled labour**, since precise alignment is built into the tooling rather than dependent on operator skill.
- Improves **accuracy and repeatability**.
- Can allow **multiple parts to be machined simultaneously** in some designs, further boosting production rate.

## Clamping Principles
Clamps should apply force **directly over a solid support/locator** (not over an unsupported span, which could cause deflection/distortion), and should not apply force in a direction that could **lift the workpiece off its locators**.

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **Jig guides the cutting tool** (via bushings, typically for drilling/reaming/tapping); **Fixture holds/locates the workpiece only** (for milling/turning/grinding/welding) and is itself **clamped to the machine**.
- **3-2-1 Principle**: 3 points on the primary surface + 2 points on the secondary surface + 1 point on the tertiary surface = 6 locating points, restraining all **6 degrees of freedom** of a rigid body.
- Jigs and fixtures are used primarily in **mass/batch production** to ensure interchangeability, speed, accuracy, and reduced dependence on operator skill.
- **Locators** position the workpiece; **Clamps** hold it firmly against the locators without disturbing that position.
- **Bushings** are a defining feature specific to **jigs** (not fixtures), guiding the drilling/reaming tool directly.
