# Alloys & Types of Steel (मिश्रधातु व इस्पात के प्रकार)

## Alloy — Basic Definition

An **alloy** is a substance composed of two or more metals (or a metal and a non-metal, most commonly carbon) mixed together to obtain properties (strength, hardness, corrosion resistance) superior to the pure constituent metals alone.

## Ferrous vs Non-Ferrous Alloys

| | Ferrous | Non-Ferrous |
|---|---|---|
| Base metal | Iron (Fe) | Metals other than iron (Al, Cu, Zn, etc.) |
| Examples | Steel, Cast Iron | Brass, Bronze, Duralumin |
| Magnetic | Generally yes | Generally no |
| Corrosion resistance | Lower (rusts) | Generally higher |

## Iron-Carbon Alloys — Classification by Carbon Content

| Material | Carbon Content | Key Property |
|---|---|---|
| **Wrought Iron** | < 0.05–0.25% C (very low) | Highly ductile, malleable, tough, easily forged/welded; used for chains, hooks, decorative work |
| **Steel (Mild/Low Carbon)** | 0.05–0.25% C | Ductile, weldable, moderate strength |
| **Medium Carbon Steel** | 0.25–0.60% C | Higher strength, less ductile; used for gears, shafts, axles |
| **High Carbon Steel** | 0.60–1.5% (up to ~2%) C | High hardness/strength, low ductility; used for cutting tools, springs, dies |
| **Cast Iron (CI)** | 2–4.5% C (typically 2–4%) | Brittle, good compressive strength, excellent castability, low melting point |

**Key memorized boundary: Steel has carbon up to ~2% (some texts cite 2.0-2.14%); above that it is classified as Cast Iron.**

## Types of Cast Iron

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Grey Cast Iron** | Carbon present as **graphite flakes**; grey fracture surface; good machinability, damping capacity; most common type; used for machine bases, engine blocks |
| **White Cast Iron** | Carbon present as **cementite (Fe₃C)**, not free graphite; very hard, brittle, white fracture surface; used where high wear resistance is needed (before further heat treatment to malleable iron) |
| **Malleable Cast Iron** | White CI heat-treated (annealed) to convert cementite into graphite nodules — gains ductility; used for pipe fittings, small machine parts |
| **Nodular / Ductile / Spheroidal Graphite (SG) Iron** | Graphite present as **spherical nodules** (via addition of magnesium/cerium during casting) — combines strength of steel with castability of cast iron; used for crankshafts, gears |
| **Chilled Cast Iron** | Surface rapidly cooled (chilled) during casting to form a hard white-iron layer over a softer grey-iron core; used for railway wheels, rolling mill rolls |

## Types of Steel — By Composition

### Plain Carbon Steel
Classified as low, medium, high carbon (see table above) — properties depend almost entirely on carbon content, no significant alloying elements.

### Alloy Steel
Contains deliberately added alloying elements (beyond carbon) to enhance specific properties.

| Alloying Element | Effect Imparted |
|---|---|
| **Chromium (Cr)** | Hardness, wear resistance, corrosion resistance |
| **Nickel (Ni)** | Toughness, impact strength, corrosion resistance |
| **Manganese (Mn)** | Hardness, wear resistance, deoxidizer; improves hot-working |
| **Molybdenum (Mo)** | High-temperature strength, hardenability |
| **Tungsten (W)** | Hardness at high temperature (retains hardness under heat — used in high-speed steel) |
| **Vanadium (V)** | Grain refinement, fatigue resistance |
| **Silicon (Si)** | Elasticity/spring properties, deoxidizer |
| **Cobalt (Co)** | Red-hardness (retains hardness at elevated temperature) |

### Stainless Steel
Iron-Carbon alloy with **minimum ~10.5–18% Chromium**, which forms a passive chromium-oxide layer that resists corrosion/rusting.

| Type | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Austenitic (e.g., 18-8: 18% Cr, 8% Ni)** | Non-magnetic, most common, highly corrosion-resistant, widely used (kitchenware, chemical plants) |
| **Ferritic** | Magnetic, moderate corrosion resistance, less nickel content |
| **Martensitic** | Magnetic, hardenable by heat treatment, used for cutlery, surgical instruments |

### High-Speed Steel (HSS)
Alloy steel (typically with W, Cr, V, Mo) used for cutting tools because it **retains hardness at high temperatures** generated during high-speed machining ("red-hardness").

### Tool Steel
High-carbon or alloy steel designed for making tools/dies — high hardness, wear resistance, ability to hold a cutting edge.

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **Steel = 0.05% to ~2% carbon**; **Cast Iron = 2% to ~4.5% carbon** — the fundamental classification boundary.
- **Wrought iron has the lowest carbon content** among the iron family (< 0.25%), giving maximum ductility/malleability.
- **Grey CI** = graphite flakes; **White CI** = cementite (very hard, brittle); **Malleable CI** = heat-treated white CI; **SG/Ductile CI** = spherical graphite nodules (Mg-treated).
- **Stainless steel corrosion resistance comes from Chromium** (minimum ~10.5-18%), forming a passive oxide layer.
- **Chromium → hardness & corrosion resistance; Nickel → toughness; Tungsten/Cobalt → red-hardness (high-speed steel); Vanadium → grain refinement.**
- **18-8 stainless steel** (18% Cr, 8% Ni) is the standard austenitic composition — very frequently asked as a specific number.

## Practice Questions

1. What is the approximate carbon content range that distinguishes steel from cast iron? → **Steel: up to ~2%; Cast Iron: above ~2% (up to ~4.5%)**
2. Which type of cast iron contains graphite in spherical nodule form? → **Nodular/Ductile/SG Iron**
3. Which alloying element is primarily responsible for the corrosion resistance of stainless steel? → **Chromium**
4. Which alloying elements give high-speed steel its "red-hardness" property? → **Tungsten and Cobalt (also Molybdenum, Vanadium contribute)**
