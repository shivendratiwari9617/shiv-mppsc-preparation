# Metal Forming Processes — Forging, Rolling, Extrusion, Drawing (धातु फॉर्मिंग प्रक्रियाएं)

## Forming vs Casting vs Machining — Category Distinction

**Metal forming** = shaping metal by **plastic deformation** (no material added or removed) — distinct from **casting** (pouring molten metal into a mould) and **machining** (removing material as chips). Forming generally gives **better mechanical properties** than casting/machining because it aligns/refines the internal **grain flow** along the part's shape rather than cutting across grains.

## Hot Working vs Cold Working — Fundamental Classification (extremely high-yield)

The dividing line is the metal's **recrystallization temperature**, not a fixed absolute temperature:

| | Hot Working | Cold Working |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | **Above** recrystallization temperature | **Below** recrystallization temperature (often room temp) |
| Force required | **Lower** (metal is soft/ductile) | **Higher** (metal is harder to deform) |
| Surface finish | Poorer (oxidation/scaling) | **Better**, more precise dimensions |
| Grain structure | Grains **recrystallize continuously** — no strain hardening, ductility retained | **Strain hardening** occurs — increases strength/hardness but reduces ductility |
| Examples | Hot rolling, hot forging, hot extrusion | Cold rolling, cold drawing, cold forging (upsetting), wire drawing |

## Forging — Types

Shaping metal by **compressive force (hammering or pressing)**, usually while hot.

- **Drop forging (die forging)**: workpiece hammered between two shaped dies (upper falls under gravity/power) — used for mass production of shaped components (e.g., connecting rods, crankshafts)
- **Press forging**: gradual squeezing pressure (not impact) between dies — deeper, more uniform deformation than hammering
- **Upset forging**: workpiece compressed along its axis to increase diameter/reduce length locally (e.g., forming bolt heads)
- **Smith forging (open-die)**: simple shapes made by hand-hammering on flat/simple dies, no closed die cavity

## Rolling

Reducing thickness (or changing cross-section) of metal by passing it between **rotating rolls**.

- **Hot rolling**: primary process for converting cast ingots into blooms/billets/slabs, then into plates/sheets/structural sections
- **Cold rolling**: gives thin sheets with good surface finish and dimensional accuracy (e.g., car body sheets)
- **Rolling mill configurations**: two-high (2 rolls), three-high (3 rolls, alternate up/down passes without reversing), four-high (2 small working rolls + 2 large backup rolls, for thin sheet), cluster mill (many backup rolls, for very thin/hard material like foil)

## Extrusion

Forcing a metal billet through a **die orifice** under high pressure to obtain a continuous product of the die's cross-sectional shape (like squeezing toothpaste).

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Direct (forward) extrusion** | Billet and extruded product move in the **same direction** as the ram |
| **Indirect (backward) extrusion** | Product moves in the **opposite direction** to the ram; billet stays stationary — **lower friction/force required** than direct extrusion, but mechanically more complex |

- **Extrusion ratio** = initial cross-sectional area / final cross-sectional area — higher ratio needs more force
- Common products: aluminium window sections, pipes, rods, structural shapes

## Drawing

Pulling metal through a **die** to reduce its cross-section (opposite of extrusion — pulled, not pushed).

- **Wire drawing**: classic example — wire pulled through a series of progressively smaller dies to reduce diameter
- **Tube drawing**: similarly reduces tube diameter/wall thickness

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **Forming (plastic deformation) is distinct from casting (solidification) and machining (material removal)** — forming preserves/improves grain flow.
- **Hot working**: above recrystallization temperature, lower force, no strain hardening. **Cold working**: below recrystallization temperature, higher force, causes strain hardening (stronger but less ductile).
- **Forging types**: drop (impact, closed die), press (gradual squeeze), upset (axial compression to increase diameter).
- **Rolling mills**: two-high, three-high, four-high, cluster — four-high and cluster used for thin/precision sheet.
- **Direct vs indirect extrusion**: direct = ram and product move same direction (higher friction); indirect = opposite direction (lower force needed).
- **Drawing** reduces cross-section by **pulling** through a die (wire drawing is the classic example) — opposite direction of force application to extrusion.

## Practice Questions

1. What determines whether a forming process is classified as "hot working" or "cold working"? → **Whether it is performed above or below the metal's recrystallization temperature**
2. Which extrusion type requires less force — direct or indirect? → **Indirect (backward) extrusion**, because there is no relative sliding friction between billet and container wall
3. Name the classic example of a "drawing" process. → **Wire drawing** (pulling wire through progressively smaller dies)
4. Why does cold working increase strength but reduce ductility? → **Strain hardening** — dislocation density increases during deformation below the recrystallization temperature, since no recrystallization occurs to relieve it
