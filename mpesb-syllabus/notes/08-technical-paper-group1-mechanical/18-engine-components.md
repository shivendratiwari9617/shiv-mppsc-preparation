# Engine Components — Piston, Crankshaft, Valve Timing (इंजन के भाग)

## Major Engine Components & Their Functions

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cylinder** | Guides the piston, contains the combustion process; usually part of the **cylinder block** |
| **Piston** | Reciprocates inside the cylinder, transmits gas pressure force to the connecting rod |
| **Piston Rings** | Fitted in grooves around the piston — seal the gap between piston and cylinder wall |
| **Connecting Rod** | Connects piston to crankshaft; converts piston's reciprocating motion into crankshaft's rotary motion |
| **Crankshaft** | Converts reciprocating motion (via connecting rod) into rotary motion; delivers output power |
| **Crankcase** | Houses/supports the crankshaft, contains lubricating oil (in wet-sump systems) |
| **Cylinder Head** | Closes the top of the cylinder; houses valves, spark plug (SI)/injector (CI), combustion chamber |
| **Camshaft** | Operates the valves (opens/closes at correct timing) via cams, lobes, and (in OHV engines) pushrods/rocker arms |
| **Flywheel** | Stores rotational kinetic energy, smooths out power delivery fluctuations between power strokes (see also point 29) |
| **Valves (Inlet & Exhaust)** | Control the flow of charge in and burnt gases out of the cylinder |

## Piston Rings — Types & Function

| Type | Function |
|---|---|
| **Compression Rings** | Upper ring(s), seal combustion gases, prevent leakage (blow-by) into the crankcase |
| **Oil Control Ring (Scraper Ring)** | Lower ring, scrapes excess lubricating oil off the cylinder wall, returns it to the sump — prevents oil from entering the combustion chamber |

## Piston Materials
Typically **cast iron** or **aluminium alloy** — aluminium alloy pistons are lighter (reduce reciprocating mass, allow higher engine speeds) and have better heat dissipation, but higher thermal expansion (requires more running clearance).

## Connecting Rod — Key Feature
- **Small end**: connects to the piston (via **gudgeon pin/piston pin**).
- **Big end**: connects to the crankshaft (via a bearing, split for assembly around the crank).

## Crankshaft — Key Features
- Made from **forged steel or cast iron**, must withstand cyclic bending and torsional loads.
- **Crank throw**: the offset of the crankpin from the main axis — determines the stroke length (stroke = 2 × crank throw/radius).
- Balanced with **counterweights** to reduce vibration from reciprocating/rotating masses (see also point 30).

## Camshaft & Valve Mechanism

- Driven by the crankshaft via a **timing belt/chain or gear train**, at **half the crankshaft speed in a 4-stroke engine** (since the camshaft completes one rotation for every 2 crankshaft rotations — matching the once-per-cycle valve operation requirement).
- **Cam profile** shape directly determines valve lift, opening duration, and timing (see also point 26 — Cams & Followers).

### Valve Train Configurations
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **OHV (Overhead Valve)** | Camshaft in block, operates valves via pushrods and rocker arms |
| **OHC (Overhead Camshaft)** | Camshaft mounted in cylinder head, directly (or via short rocker) operates valves |
| **DOHC (Double/Dual Overhead Camshaft)** | Two camshafts in head — typically one for intake, one for exhaust valves |

## Valve Timing Diagram — Key Concepts

Actual valve opening/closing does **not** occur exactly at TDC/BDC — valves are opened slightly **before** and closed slightly **after** the theoretical dead centre points, to improve breathing (volumetric efficiency) at running speeds. Key timing events:

| Event | Abbreviation | Typical timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Inlet Valve Opens** | IVO | Slightly **before** TDC (during exhaust stroke) |
| **Inlet Valve Closes** | IVC | Slightly **after** BDC (into compression stroke) |
| **Exhaust Valve Opens** | EVO | Slightly **before** BDC (during power stroke) |
| **Exhaust Valve Closes** | EVC | Slightly **after** TDC (into suction stroke) |

### Valve Overlap
The short period around TDC (between exhaust stroke ending and suction stroke beginning) during which **both inlet and exhaust valves are open simultaneously** — improves scavenging (helps push out remaining exhaust gas using incoming fresh charge's momentum) and helps cool the exhaust valve.

## Spark Plug (SI engines) vs Fuel Injector (CI engines)
- **Spark Plug**: located in cylinder head, produces a high-voltage electric spark to ignite the air-fuel mixture (petrol engines).
- **Fuel Injector**: located in cylinder head, sprays high-pressure fuel directly into the compressed hot air (diesel engines) — no spark needed, ignition is by compression heat.

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **Connecting rod**: small end → piston (via gudgeon pin), big end → crankshaft.
- **Camshaft rotates at half the crankshaft speed** in a 4-stroke engine (1 cam rotation per 2 crank rotations).
- **Compression rings** seal combustion gases; **oil control ring** scrapes/manages lubricating oil — a commonly confused pair.
- **Valve overlap**: period when both intake and exhaust valves are open together, near TDC at the end of exhaust/start of suction — improves scavenging.
- Valves open/close **slightly before/after** the exact TDC/BDC points, not exactly at them — this is deliberate, for better breathing at speed.
- Flywheel stores kinetic energy to smooth power delivery (detailed further in point 29).

## Practice Questions

1. Which engine component converts the piston's reciprocating motion into the crankshaft's rotary motion? → **Connecting rod (working with the crankshaft)**
2. At what speed does the camshaft rotate relative to the crankshaft in a 4-stroke engine? → **Half the crankshaft speed**
3. What is "valve overlap"? → **The period during which both inlet and exhaust valves are open simultaneously, near TDC**
4. Which piston ring is responsible for controlling/scraping excess lubricating oil from the cylinder wall? → **Oil control (scraper) ring**
