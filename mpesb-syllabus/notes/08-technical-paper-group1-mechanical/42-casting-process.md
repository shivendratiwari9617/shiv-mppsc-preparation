# Casting Process (कास्टिंग प्रक्रिया)

## Casting — Basic Definition

A manufacturing process in which **molten metal is poured into a mould cavity** (shaped as a hollow replica of the desired part) and allowed to **solidify**, then removed (the mould broken away or opened) to obtain the final (or near-final) shaped part — one of the oldest and most versatile manufacturing processes, suited to complex shapes not easily made by other methods.

## Sand Casting — the Most Common/Basic Casting Process

### Key Terms in Sand Casting

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | A replica of the part to be cast, slightly **oversized** (to allow for metal shrinkage on cooling) — used to form the mould cavity in the sand; typically made of wood, metal, or plastic |
| **Mould** | The cavity (formed in sand, using the pattern) into which molten metal is poured |
| **Core** | A separate sand shape inserted into the mould to create **internal cavities/holes** in the casting that the pattern alone cannot produce |
| **Cope** | The **upper (top) half** of the sand mould/flask |
| **Drag** | The **lower (bottom) half** of the sand mould/flask |
| **Flask** | The rigid frame/box that holds/contains the sand mould (cope + drag) |
| **Gating System** | The complete network of channels through which molten metal flows into the mould cavity |
| **Sprue** | The main **vertical** channel through which molten metal first enters the gating system from the pouring basin |
| **Runner** | **Horizontal** channel(s) that carry molten metal from the sprue to the individual gates |
| **Gate (Ingate)** | The final, small opening through which molten metal actually enters the mould cavity itself |
| **Riser (Feeder Head)** | An additional reservoir of molten metal, connected to the casting, that **feeds molten metal back into the casting as it shrinks during solidification** — compensates for shrinkage, prevents shrinkage cavities/porosity in the main casting |
| **Parting Line** | The line/plane along which the mould (cope and drag) separates |

### Pattern Allowances (why the pattern is NOT the exact final size)

| Allowance | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Shrinkage Allowance** | Compensates for the metal's **contraction on cooling** (solidification shrinkage + solid contraction) — pattern is made **larger** than the finished part by this amount |
| **Machining (Finish) Allowance** | Extra material left on surfaces that will later be **machined** to final size/finish |
| **Draft (Taper) Allowance** | A slight **taper** given to vertical surfaces of the pattern, to allow it to be **withdrawn/lifted cleanly** from the sand mould without damaging the mould cavity walls |
| **Distortion (Camber) Allowance** | Compensates for **warping** that may occur in certain shapes (e.g., U-shaped or long, thin castings) as they cool unevenly |
| **Shake (Rapping) Allowance** | A small **reduction** applied because the pattern is slightly shaken/rapped before withdrawal (to loosen it in the sand), which enlarges the cavity very slightly |

## Casting Defects (commonly tested)

| Defect | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Blow Holes (Gas Porosity)** | Trapped gas/moisture in the sand, escaping into the molten metal during pouring |
| **Shrinkage Cavity** | Insufficient feeding (riser) during solidification, leaving a void as the metal shrinks |
| **Cold Shut** | Two streams of molten metal meet but **fail to fuse properly** (metal too cool by the time streams meet) — leaves a visible discontinuity/crack-like defect |
| **Misrun** | Molten metal **solidifies before completely filling** the mould cavity (insufficient fluidity/pouring temperature or too-thin sections) |
| **Sand Inclusion / Dross** | Loose sand or slag/impurities gets trapped and embedded in the casting |
| **Porosity** | Small gas bubbles trapped throughout the casting |

## Other Casting Processes (beyond basic sand casting)

| Process | Description |
|---|---|
| **Die Casting** | Molten metal (typically low-melting-point non-ferrous alloys — aluminium, zinc) forced under **high pressure** into a reusable **metal die/mould** — excellent surface finish and dimensional accuracy, high production rate, suited to mass production of small-to-medium parts |
| **Investment Casting (Lost-Wax Process)** | A wax pattern is coated with a ceramic shell; the wax is then melted out ("lost"), leaving a ceramic mould cavity into which metal is poured — gives excellent surface finish and very intricate detail, but a slower, costlier process, suited to complex/precision parts (e.g., turbine blades, jewellery) |
| **Centrifugal Casting** | Molten metal is poured into a **rotating** mould — centrifugal force distributes/packs the metal against the mould wall, well-suited to producing **hollow, cylindrical/symmetric parts** (e.g., pipes) with dense, defect-free structure (impurities, being lighter, migrate toward the centre/bore, away from the working material) |
| **Permanent Mould (Gravity Die) Casting** | Similar to die casting but metal fills the reusable metal mould **under gravity alone** (not forced pressure) — better finish than sand casting but at lower pressure/production rate than die casting |
| **Shell Moulding** | A thin, precisely-formed shell of resin-bonded sand is used as the mould, formed against a heated metal pattern — better surface finish and accuracy than plain sand casting |

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **Cope** = upper mould half; **Drag** = lower mould half.
- **Riser** compensates for **shrinkage during solidification**; **Core** creates **internal cavities/holes** — two very frequently confused terms with distinct purposes.
- **Sprue** (vertical, main entry) → **Runner** (horizontal) → **Gate** (final entry into cavity) — the correct order of the gating system.
- Pattern is made **larger** than the finished part (shrinkage allowance) and has a slight **taper (draft)** for easy withdrawal from the sand.
- **Die casting**: high pressure, reusable metal die, mass production of small non-ferrous parts. **Investment (lost-wax) casting**: wax pattern melted away, excellent finish/detail, used for complex/precision parts. **Centrifugal casting**: rotating mould, ideal for hollow cylindrical parts like pipes.

## Practice Questions

1. What is the purpose of a riser in sand casting? → **To feed molten metal back into the casting as it shrinks during solidification, preventing shrinkage cavities**
2. What is the purpose of a core in sand casting? → **To create internal cavities/holes in the casting that the pattern alone cannot form**
3. Why is a pattern made slightly larger than the finished cast part? → **To compensate for shrinkage of the metal as it cools/solidifies (shrinkage allowance)**
4. Which casting process is best suited for producing hollow cylindrical parts like pipes, using a rotating mould? → **Centrifugal casting**
