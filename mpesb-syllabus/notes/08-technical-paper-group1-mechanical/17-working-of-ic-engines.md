# Working of IC Engines — Petrol & Diesel, 2-Stroke/4-Stroke (पेट्रोल व डीजल इंजन कार्यप्रणाली)

## Basic Classification of IC Engines

| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Fuel/Ignition | **SI (Spark Ignition)** — petrol; **CI (Compression Ignition)** — diesel |
| Number of strokes | **2-Stroke**; **4-Stroke** |
| Cylinder arrangement | In-line, V-type, Radial, Opposed |
| Cooling | Air-cooled, Water-cooled |

## Basic Engine Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Top Dead Centre (TDC)** | Position of piston nearest the cylinder head (minimum volume) |
| **Bottom Dead Centre (BDC)** | Position of piston farthest from cylinder head (maximum volume) |
| **Stroke** | Distance travelled by piston between TDC and BDC |
| **Bore** | Internal diameter of the cylinder |
| **Swept/Displacement Volume** | Volume swept by piston between TDC and BDC |
| **Clearance Volume** | Volume remaining in cylinder when piston is at TDC |
| **Compression Ratio (r)** | r = (Swept Volume + Clearance Volume) / Clearance Volume |

## Four-Stroke Engine Cycle (both petrol & diesel follow this same 4-event sequence; only ignition method differs)

| Stroke | Piston Motion | Valves | Event |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Suction (Intake)** | TDC → BDC | Inlet valve open, exhaust closed | Fresh air (diesel) or air-fuel mixture (petrol) drawn in |
| **2. Compression** | BDC → TDC | Both valves closed | Charge compressed; near TDC — spark ignites mixture (petrol) OR fuel injected & self-ignites due to heat of compression (diesel) |
| **3. Power (Expansion)** | TDC → BDC | Both valves closed | Burning gases expand, pushing piston down — the only stroke that produces net work output |
| **4. Exhaust** | BDC → TDC | Exhaust valve open, inlet closed | Burnt gases expelled from cylinder |

**Crankshaft rotation for one complete cycle = 2 revolutions (720°)** in a 4-stroke engine — a frequently asked fact.

## Two-Stroke Engine Cycle

Completes the same 4 events (suction, compression, power, exhaust) but in only **2 strokes of the piston (1 crankshaft revolution = 360°)**, by combining/overlapping events using **ports** (instead of mechanically operated valves) that are opened/closed by the piston's own motion:

| Stroke | Events combined |
|---|---|
| **1st stroke (Upward, BDC→TDC)** | Compression of charge above piston + simultaneous fresh charge induction into crankcase below piston |
| **2nd stroke (Downward, TDC→BDC)** | Power (expansion) + exhaust port opens + transfer port opens, allowing crankcase charge to sweep into cylinder while pushing out exhaust gases ("scavenging") |

### Ports in a 2-stroke engine
**Inlet port, Transfer port, Exhaust port** — opened/closed purely by piston position, no separate valve mechanism needed.

## 2-Stroke vs 4-Stroke — Comparison (very high-yield)

| | 2-Stroke | 4-Stroke |
|---|---|---|
| Power stroke per revolution | 1 power stroke per crankshaft revolution | 1 power stroke per 2 crankshaft revolutions |
| Power output (same size, same rpm) | Theoretically higher (more frequent power strokes) | Lower |
| Mechanism | Simpler (no valve gear — uses ports) | More complex (needs valve mechanism — camshaft, valves) |
| Lubrication (petrol 2-stroke) | Often mixed with fuel (petroil), leads to more pollution | Separate lubrication system (sump/wet or dry sump) |
| Fuel efficiency | Lower (some fresh charge escapes with exhaust — "short-circuiting") | Higher |
| Cooling/thermal loading | More per unit time (more frequent combustion) | Less |
| Common application | Small engines — mopeds, small generators, chainsaws | Cars, trucks, most modern motorcycles |

## SI (Petrol) vs CI (Diesel) Engine — Comparison (very high-yield)

| | SI (Petrol) Engine | CI (Diesel) Engine |
|---|---|---|
| Ignition | Spark plug ignites air-fuel mixture | Self-ignition due to heat of compression |
| Fuel supplied | Air-fuel mixture inducted together (carburetor/port injection) | Only air inducted; fuel injected directly near end of compression |
| Compression ratio | Lower (6–10) — limited by knocking | Higher (14–22) |
| Thermal efficiency | Lower | Higher (due to higher compression ratio) |
| Cycle followed (ideal) | Otto Cycle (constant volume heat addition) | Diesel Cycle (constant pressure heat addition) |
| Weight/cost | Lighter, cheaper | Heavier, costlier (must withstand higher pressures) |
| Speed | Generally higher rpm capability | Generally lower rpm |

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **4-stroke engine**: 1 power stroke per **2 crankshaft revolutions (720°)**; **2-stroke engine**: 1 power stroke per **1 crankshaft revolution (360°)**.
- The four events (suction, compression, power, exhaust) occur in **every** IC engine, whether 2-stroke or 4-stroke — 2-stroke just overlaps/combines them using ports instead of mechanical valves.
- **Petrol/SI engine**: spark ignition, air-fuel mixture inducted together, Otto cycle, lower compression ratio.
- **Diesel/CI engine**: compression ignition (self-ignition), only air inducted (fuel injected separately), Diesel cycle, higher compression ratio, higher thermal efficiency.
- The **power stroke** is the only stroke producing net positive work in the 4-stroke cycle.
- 2-stroke engines are mechanically simpler (no valve gear) but less fuel-efficient due to charge loss during scavenging.

## Practice Questions

1. How many crankshaft revolutions correspond to one complete cycle in a 4-stroke engine? → **2 revolutions (720°)**
2. In a diesel engine, what causes ignition of the fuel? → **Heat generated by compression of air (self-ignition), not a spark**
3. Which type of engine typically has a higher compression ratio — SI or CI? → **CI (Diesel)**
4. In a 2-stroke engine, what mechanism replaces the mechanically-operated valves of a 4-stroke engine? → **Ports (inlet, transfer, exhaust) opened/closed by piston motion**
