# Parts of Speech — Noun, Pronoun, Adjective, Verb, Adverb, Preposition, Conjunction, Interjection

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension (MPESB Prelims Subject-2) | **Section:** Grammar Fundamentals | **Exam relevance:** MPESB along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — Parts of speech identification is the foundation for nearly every other English grammar topic in this checklist (tense, agreement, voice, error-spotting all build on this).

---

## 1. What are Parts of Speech?

Words are classified into **8 categories** based on the function they perform in a sentence. Correctly identifying a word's part of speech is the base skill needed for almost every other grammar topic.

---

## 2. The 8 Parts of Speech

### (a) Noun — names a person, place, thing, animal, or idea
| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Proper | Specific name | Ram, Delhi, Ganga |
| Common | General class name | boy, city, river |
| Collective | Group as one unit | team, army, committee |
| Material | Substance/matter | gold, water, wood |
| Abstract | Quality, idea, feeling (cannot be touched) | honesty, beauty, courage |
| Countable/Uncountable | Can/cannot be counted individually | books (countable), water (uncountable) |

### (b) Pronoun — replaces a noun
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Personal | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| Possessive | mine, yours, his, hers, ours |
| Reflexive | myself, himself, themselves |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those |
| Interrogative | who, what, which, whose |
| Relative | who, which, that, whom |
| Indefinite | someone, anybody, everything, nobody |

### (c) Adjective — describes/modifies a noun or pronoun
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Descriptive/Qualitative | beautiful, tall, honest |
| Quantitative | some, much, little |
| Numeral | one, first, several |
| Demonstrative | this, that (used before a noun: "this book") |
| Possessive | my, your, his, our |
| Interrogative | which, whose, what (used before a noun) |

### (d) Verb — expresses action or state of being
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Main/Action verb | run, eat, write |
| Linking verb | is, seem, become |
| Auxiliary/Helping verb | is, have, will, can |
| Transitive (needs an object) | She **reads** a book. |
| Intransitive (no object needed) | She **sleeps**. |

### (e) Adverb — modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Manner | quickly, slowly, carefully |
| Time | now, yesterday, soon |
| Place | here, there, everywhere |
| Frequency | always, never, often |
| Degree | very, too, quite |

### (f) Preposition — shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and the rest of the sentence
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Time | at, on, in ("at 5 PM", "on Monday", "in 2024") |
| Place | in, on, at, under, between, among |
| Direction | to, towards, into |

### (g) Conjunction — connects words, phrases, or clauses
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Coordinating (FANBOYS) | for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so |
| Subordinating | because, although, if, when, since |
| Correlative | either...or, neither...nor, both...and |

### (h) Interjection — expresses sudden emotion
Examples: Oh!, Wow!, Alas!, Hurrah!, Ouch!

---

## 3. Commonly Confused Distinctions

| Confusion | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Adjective vs Adverb | Adjective modifies a **noun/pronoun** ("a quick runner"); Adverb modifies a **verb/adjective/adverb** ("runs quickly"). |
| Preposition vs Conjunction | Preposition connects a **noun/pronoun** to the rest of the sentence ("before the meeting"); Conjunction connects **clauses** ("before he left"). |
| Transitive vs Intransitive verb | Transitive needs an object to complete meaning ("She **wrote** a letter"); Intransitive is complete without one ("She **wrote**" — needs no object for grammatical completeness, though it can still take one). |
| Same word, different part of speech | Many words change part of speech by context — e.g., "**round**" can be a noun (a round of golf), adjective (a round table), verb (to round a corner), preposition (round the corner), or adverb (turn round). |

## 4. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Identify the part of speech: "Honesty is the best policy." (Honesty) | Abstract Noun |
| "She sings **beautifully**." — part of speech of the bolded word | Adverb (manner) |
| "The book is **on** the table." — part of speech of "on" | Preposition |
| "He was tired **but** he continued working." — part of speech of "but" | Conjunction (coordinating) |
| "**Alas!** She has failed." — part of speech of "Alas" | Interjection |
| "**Neither** John **nor** Mary came." — type of conjunction | Correlative conjunction |

---

## 5. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. Learn all **8 categories and their sub-types** cold — this is the base every later topic (tense, agreement, voice, error-spotting) assumes you already know.
2. Master the **adjective vs adverb** and **preposition vs conjunction** distinctions — these are the most commonly tested confusions.
3. Practice identifying the part of speech of the **same word used differently in different sentences** (like "round") — this exact trick appears often in objective papers.
4. This topic feeds directly into point 22 (Word formation — prefixes and suffixes) and point 14 (Spotting errors) — a solid grip here makes both of those faster.
