# Reading Comprehension — Unseen Passages with Inference-Based Questions

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Comprehension (Skill-Based) | **Exam relevance:** MPESB along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — this parallels Hindi points 21-22 (अपठित गद्यांश/पद्यांश); English papers additionally emphasize **inference-based** questions, which need a distinct skill from literal recall.

---

## 1. What is Reading Comprehension?

An unseen passage is given, followed by questions testing your understanding at multiple levels — not just what the passage says directly, but also what it implies.

---

## 2. Types of Questions

| Type | Tests |
|---|---|
| Literal/factual | Directly stated information ("What did X do?") |
| **Inferential** | Implied meaning not directly stated — must be logically deduced |
| Vocabulary-in-context | The meaning of a word **as used in this specific passage** (may differ from its usual dictionary meaning) |
| Main idea/theme/title | The central point of the whole passage |
| Author's tone/attitude | Critical, appreciative, neutral, sarcastic, objective |
| Passage structure | How the argument/narrative is organized |

---

## 3. Literal vs Inferential — the Key Distinction

| Literal question | Inferential question |
|---|---|
| "What did X do?" — answer is directly stated in the text | "Why did X do this?" / "What can we conclude about X's motivation?" — requires reading between the lines |

**Rule for inference questions:** the correct answer must be a **logical, reasonable extension of what's actually stated** — not something entirely new, and not something merely "generally true" but unsupported by *this* passage.

---

## 4. Strategy — Step by Step

1. **Skim the passage first** for overall gist — don't get bogged down in details on the first read.
2. **Read the questions** to know what specifically to look for.
3. **Re-read relevant portions carefully** for each question rather than relying on memory.
4. For **inference questions**, look only for clues within the passage — don't import outside knowledge or assumptions.
5. For **tone questions**, note the adjectives/adverbs the author chooses, and whether facts are stated neutrally or with clear judgment.

---

## 5. Common Inference Traps

- Choosing an option that's **true in general** but not actually supported by *this specific* passage.
- **Over-inference** — choosing an option that goes further than what the passage actually implies.
- Choosing an option that **contradicts** a detail stated in the passage.

---

## 6. Worked Example

> "Many people believe that success comes only through hard work. However, history shows us that opportunity often plays an equally important role. Two equally talented individuals may have vastly different outcomes depending on the circumstances they are born into. This does not mean hard work is unimportant — rather, it suggests that success is the product of both effort and circumstance working together."

| Question | Answer (with reasoning) |
|---|---|
| Main idea? | Success depends on **both** hard work and opportunity, not hard work alone. |
| Inference: The author would most likely agree that... | Both effort and opportunity are necessary for success. (The passage explicitly rejects "hard work alone" and never claims circumstance alone determines success — it argues both together.) |
| Author's tone? | Balanced/objective — the author validates both factors rather than dismissing either. |
| "Circumstances" in this passage most nearly means... | The conditions/situation a person is born into (context makes this clear, even without a dictionary). |

---

## 7. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

Using the worked passage above:
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is "hard work is unimportant" a valid inference from this passage? | No — the passage explicitly denies this ("This does not mean hard work is unimportant") |
| Does the passage claim circumstance alone determines success? | No — it argues effort **and** circumstance work together |

---

## 8. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. **Inferential questions are not guesswork** — the correct answer is always traceable to specific language in the passage; if you can't point to the supporting sentence, you've likely over-inferred.
2. Always check inference options against the passage's **explicit denials** — passages often pre-empt the wrong answer by directly rejecting it (as in the worked example above).
3. **Vocabulary-in-context** questions should be answered from the passage's usage, not a memorized dictionary definition — a word can carry a special meaning within the specific passage.
4. This topic shares its core strategy with Hindi points 21-22 — skim first, read questions, then re-read for specifics; the skill transfers directly between languages.
