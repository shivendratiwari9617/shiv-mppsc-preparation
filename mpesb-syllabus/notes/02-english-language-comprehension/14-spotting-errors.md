# Spotting Errors (Error Detection in Sentences)

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar Application (Skill-Based) | **Exam relevance:** MPESB along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — this is a **consolidation topic**: a sentence is split into segments, and you must identify which segment has an error (or mark "No error"). It draws directly on points 1-9 plus several new error categories introduced here.

---

## 1. Format of This Question Type

A sentence is divided into 3-4 parts (e.g., A / B / C / D), sometimes with a "No error" option (E). You identify which single part contains a grammatical mistake.

## 3. New Error Categories (Introduced Here)

### (a) Pronoun Case Errors
Pronouns must be in the correct case: **subjective** (I, he, she, we, they) for subjects; **objective** (me, him, her, us, them) after verbs/prepositions.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| Between you and **I**. | Between you and **me**. (object of preposition "between") |
| **Him** and **me** went to the market. | **He** and **I** went to the market. (subject needs nominative case) |

### (b) Comparison Errors (cross-reference point 24)
| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| He is **more taller** than me. | He is **taller** than me. (double comparative) |
| This is the **most best** option. | This is the **best** option. (double superlative) |

### (c) Redundancy/Tautology
Using two words that repeat the same meaning.
| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| He **returned back** home. | He returned home. ("return" already implies "back") |
| The reason is **because** he was late. | The reason is **that** he was late. |
| **Repeat again**. | Repeat. ("repeat" already implies "again") |

### (d) Parallelism Errors
All items in a list/series must share the **same grammatical form**.
| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| She likes reading, writing, and **to paint**. | She likes reading, writing, and **painting**. (all gerunds) |

### (e) Commonly Confused Word Pairs
| Pair | Rule |
|---|---|
| fewer vs less | fewer = countable nouns ("fewer books"); less = uncountable nouns ("less water") |
| each other vs one another | each other = between 2 people; one another = among more than 2 |
| number vs amount | number = countable ("a number of students"); amount = uncountable ("an amount of money") |

## 4. Systematic Error-Checking Strategy

Apply these checks **in order** to any sentence:

1. **Subject-verb agreement** — does the verb match the true subject in number? (most common error)
2. **Tense consistency** — do all verbs in the sentence make logical time sense together?
3. **Articles and prepositions** — any missing/wrong a/an/the, or a fixed preposition misused?
4. **Pronoun case and agreement** — subjective vs objective case; pronoun matches its antecedent in number/gender?
5. **Redundancy and parallelism** — any repeated meaning, or a mismatched list structure?
6. If none of the above apply after a careful pass, consider **"No error."**

---

## 5. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Sentence (error segment marked) | Correction |
|---|---|
| "He is / one of the / best player / in the team." | "best **players**" (superlative + "one of the" needs plural noun) |
| "Neither of the students / were / present." | "was" (Rule 5, point 3 — "neither" is singular) |
| "She is / senior than / me." | "senior **to**" (fixed comparison preposition, not "than") |
| "He said that / the sun / rose in the east." | "**rises**" (universal truth — no tense backshift, point 5) |

---

## 6. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. This topic is a **direct test of points 1-9 combined** — the stronger your grasp of those, the faster you'll spot errors here.
2. Run the **systematic checklist (Section 4)** in order rather than reading randomly — subject-verb agreement and tense are the highest-probability error locations, so check those first.
3. **Redundancy errors (Section 3c)** are easy to miss because the sentence "sounds fine" — train yourself to ask "does any word repeat a meaning already present?"
4. Don't over-correct — if a sentence is genuinely fine, **"No error" is a valid and commonly correct answer**; don't force-find a mistake that isn't there.
