# Prepositions — Common Usage Errors and Fixed Prepositions

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar Fundamentals | **Exam relevance:** MPESB along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — "fill in the correct preposition" and "spot the preposition error" are among the most frequently repeated question types because English prepositions don't follow predictable logic and must largely be memorized with their paired verb/adjective.

---

## 1. Prepositions of Time

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| at | specific point in time | at 5 o'clock, at night, at noon |
| on | days/dates | on Monday, on 15th August |
| in | months/years/seasons/longer periods | in June, in 2024, in winter, in the morning |

## 2. Prepositions of Place

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| at | a specific point | at the door, at the corner |
| on | a surface | on the table, on the wall |
| in | an enclosed space | in the room, in the box |

## 3. Prepositions of Direction/Movement

to, towards, into, onto, from, through, across, along

## 4. Fixed (Idiomatic) Prepositions — Verb + Preposition (High-Yield)

| Verb + Preposition | Verb + Preposition |
|---|---|
| depend **on/upon** | rely **on** |
| agree **with** (a person) | agree **to** (a proposal) |
| agree **on** (a matter) | apply **for** (a job) |
| apply **to** (a situation) | listen **to** |
| look **at** (watch) | look **after** (take care of) |
| look **for** (search) | look **into** (investigate) |
| consist **of** | deal **with** |
| succeed **in** | believe **in** |
| congratulate **on** | compare **to** (similarity) |
| compare **with** (contrast) | complain **about/of** |
| differ **from** (a thing) | differ **with** (a person, opinion) |
| deprive **of** | indulge **in** |
| insist **on** | object **to** |
| prevent **from** | rejoice **at/over** |
| result **in** (cause → effect) | result **from** (effect ← cause) |
| subscribe **to** | part **with** (a thing) |
| part **from** (a person) | — |

## 6. Common Preposition Errors — Verbs That Take NO Preposition

A classic exam trap: certain transitive verbs are followed **directly** by their object with **no preposition at all**, even though learners often add one by habit.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| We discussed **about** the matter. | We discussed the matter. |
| I requested **to** him. | I requested him. |
| He reached **to** the station. | He reached the station. |
| He resembles **to/with** his father. | He resembles his father. |
| He married **with** her. | He married her. (but: "He **is married to** her" — adjective use is different) |

## 7. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Sentence | Correct preposition |
|---|---|
| He is good ___ Mathematics. | at |
| She is fond ___ music. | of |
| The result depends ___ your effort. | on |
| He succeeded ___ passing the exam. | in |
| They complained ___ the poor service. | about/of |
| This book differs ___ that one. (a thing) | from |
| I differ ___ you on this issue. (a person, opinion) | with |
| We discussed the plan. — is "about" needed? | No — "discuss" takes no preposition |

---

## 8. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. **Fixed verb/adjective + preposition combinations (Sections 4, 5)** must simply be memorized — there's no reliable logical rule, and this is the single biggest scoring category in preposition questions.
2. **No-preposition verbs (Section 6)** — discuss, request, reach, resemble, marry — are a near-guaranteed error-spotting trap; a wrongly added preposition after these is one of the most repeated mistakes tested.
3. Pay special attention to **pairs where the preposition changes the meaning**: compare to/with, differ from/with, result in/from, angry with/at — these test precise understanding, not just memorization.
4. This topic feeds directly into point 14 (Spotting errors) and point 16 (Fill in the blanks) — preposition questions are one of the most common formats in both.
