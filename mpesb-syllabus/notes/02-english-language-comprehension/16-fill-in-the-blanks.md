# Fill in the Blanks — Vocabulary and Grammar Based

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar & Vocabulary Application | **Exam relevance:** MPESB along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — this format directly tests everything from points 1-13 in a single-blank or double-blank sentence, plus **collocations** (word pairings), which is new ground covered here.

---

## 1. Two Types of Fill-in-the-Blank Questions

| Type | Tests | Draws on |
|---|---|---|
| **Grammar-based** | Correct verb form, preposition, article, conjunction, modal | Points 1-9 |
| **Vocabulary-based** | Correct word choice among close synonyms, or fixed word pairings (collocations) | Points 10-13, and Section 2 below |

---

## 2. Collocations — Words That Habitually Go Together (New Content)

A **collocation** is a natural, fixed pairing of words — even a grammatically valid alternative can sound wrong to a native speaker if it breaks the expected pairing. This is a distinct skill from grammar rules and is tested frequently.

### "Make" vs "Do" — the Most Common Collocation Confusion
| Do (tasks, duties, work in general) | Make (creating/producing, or fixed set phrases) |
|---|---|
| do homework | make a decision |
| do the dishes | make a mistake |
| do a favor | make money |
| do business | make noise |
| do research | make progress |
| do the shopping | make an effort |

### Other High-Frequency Collocations
| Collocation |
|---|
| pay attention |
| take a break |
| have a look |
| take responsibility |
| keep a promise / break a promise |
| catch a cold |
| lose weight / gain weight |
| take a photo |
| make a phone call |
| take a chance |
| make sense |
| give advice (never "an advice" — advice is uncountable) |
| get married |

---

## 3. Strategy for Single-Blank Grammar Questions

1. Identify **what kind of word** is missing (verb form? preposition? article?).
2. Apply the relevant rule set from points 1-9.
3. If multiple options are grammatically valid, choose based on **collocation/idiom** (Section 2).

---

## 4. Strategy for Double-Blank Questions

Some sentences have **two blanks that must work together logically** — read both blanks as a pair before choosing, since options are often given as matched pairs (e.g., "unable...sudden").

**Example:** "The mayor was ___ to attend the meeting due to a ___ crisis."
Correct pair: **unable** ... **sudden** (both blanks must make sense together, not independently).

---

## 5. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Sentence | Correct answer |
|---|---|
| "Please ___ attention while I explain this." | pay |
| "She wants to ___ a decision quickly." | make |
| "He always ___ his homework on time." | does |
| "It is important to ___ a promise once made." | keep |
| "Advice" — can you say "an advice"? | No — "advice" is uncountable; say "a piece of advice" |

---

## 6. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. **"Make vs Do" (Section 2)** is the single most tested collocation pattern — memorize the fixed-phrase lists directly, since the choice isn't governed by a logical rule.
2. When a blank has multiple grammatically correct options, the deciding factor is usually **which one is idiomatic/collocational**, not which one "also works" — go with the fixed expression.
3. For double-blank questions, **never fill the first blank in isolation** — check that your choice for blank 1 still makes sense with your choice for blank 2.
4. This topic is essentially an applied test of points 1-13 combined — strong grammar and vocabulary fundamentals make this the fastest-scoring section once mastered.
