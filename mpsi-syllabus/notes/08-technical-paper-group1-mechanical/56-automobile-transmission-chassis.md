# Automobile Transmission & Chassis (ऑटोमोबाइल ट्रांसमिशन व चेसिस)

## Chassis — Basic Definition

The **base frame** of a vehicle, onto which the engine, transmission, suspension, wheels, and (in a body-on-frame design) the body are all mounted — provides the structural rigidity and load-carrying backbone of the vehicle.

### Chassis Construction Types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Body-on-Frame (Conventional/Ladder Frame)** | A separate, rigid frame (chassis) carries all mechanical components; the body is mounted **on top of** and bolted to this frame — traditional design, still used for trucks, SUVs, buses (favoured for high load-carrying capacity, torsional strength, and ease of repair/modification) |
| **Unitary (Monocoque) Construction** | The vehicle's **body itself forms the structural frame** — no separate chassis frame; body panels/shell provide the structural strength — lighter weight, more common in modern passenger cars, gives better fuel efficiency but is generally less suited to very heavy-duty applications |

## Automobile Transmission System — Purpose & Overview

The system of components that transmits power from the engine to the driven wheels, while allowing the necessary **speed and torque conversion** (since an IC engine's usable power/torque band is narrow, but a vehicle needs a wide range of torque/speed depending on driving conditions — starting, hill-climbing, cruising).

## Clutch (recap link — point 37)
The first component in the drivetrain, allowing the engine to be **disconnected from the transmission** momentarily (for gear changes, starting from rest) — see point 37 for detailed clutch types.

## Gearbox (Transmission Box)

### Purpose
Provides a range of **gear ratios**, allowing the driver (or, in an automatic, the system itself) to select the appropriate torque-speed trade-off for current driving conditions — **low gears** give high torque/low speed (for starting, climbing), **high gears** give low torque/high speed (for cruising, fuel economy).

### Types of Gearboxes
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Manual Transmission (Sliding/Constant Mesh/Synchromesh)** | Driver manually selects gear via a clutch pedal and gear lever; **Synchromesh** mechanism (in modern manual gearboxes) allows smooth, clash-free gear changes by matching the rotational speed of gears before engagement |
| **Automatic Transmission** | Gear changes happen automatically, without driver/clutch-pedal intervention — traditionally uses a **torque converter** (a fluid coupling with additional torque-multiplication capability, replacing the mechanical friction clutch) combined with an epicyclic (planetary) gear train (point 27) for automatic ratio changes |
| **CVT (Continuously Variable Transmission)** | Provides a **stepless, continuously variable** gear ratio (not fixed discrete steps), typically using a belt/chain running between two variable-diameter pulleys — allows the engine to run at its most efficient speed continuously |
| **AMT (Automated Manual Transmission)** | Mechanically a standard manual gearbox, but the clutch and gear-shifting are operated **automatically** by actuators (electro-hydraulic/electro-mechanical), without a clutch pedal for the driver |

## Propeller Shaft & Universal Joint
In a **rear-wheel-drive (or 4WD)** vehicle layout, the **propeller shaft** transmits rotary power from the gearbox to the rear axle/differential — since the relative angle/position between the gearbox output and the rear axle can change (due to suspension movement), **Universal (Hooke's) Joints** (point 38) are fitted at each end of the propeller shaft to accommodate this changing angular relationship.

## Differential

### Purpose
Allows the two driven wheels (on the same axle) to rotate at **different speeds** while still both receiving driving torque — essential because, when a vehicle turns a corner, the **outer wheel must travel a greater distance (and hence rotate faster) than the inner wheel**; without a differential, one wheel would be forced to skid.

### Basic Working
A set of bevel gears (typically) allows torque to be split and delivered to both half-shafts (and hence both wheels), while permitting them to rotate at different speeds when required (straight-line driving: both wheels rotate at the same speed; cornering: speeds automatically differ) — the differential itself is a specific application of the **epicyclic/bevel gear train** concept.

### Limited Slip Differential (LSD) — brief mention
A modified differential that **limits** the speed difference it will allow between the two wheels, to prevent excessive torque loss when one wheel loses traction (e.g., on ice/mud) — a standard differential would send all torque to the spinning (low-traction) wheel, leaving the vehicle stuck; an LSD partially resists this.

## Drive Layouts (brief classification)

| Layout | Description |
|---|---|
| **FWD (Front-Wheel Drive)** | Engine drives the front wheels — common in modern compact/mid-size passenger cars, generally more compact and fuel-efficient packaging |
| **RWD (Rear-Wheel Drive)** | Engine drives the rear wheels (via propeller shaft + differential, as above) — traditional layout, still common in trucks, performance/luxury cars |
| **4WD/AWD (Four/All-Wheel Drive)** | Engine drives all four wheels (via additional differentials/transfer case) — better traction, common in SUVs, off-road, and performance vehicles |

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **Body-on-frame (ladder frame)**: separate structural chassis, body mounted on top — heavy-duty vehicles. **Unitary (monocoque)**: body itself is the structural frame — lighter, most modern passenger cars.
- **Gearbox** converts engine's narrow torque-speed band into the wider range needed for driving — **low gear = high torque/low speed; high gear = low torque/high speed**.
- **Synchromesh** enables smooth, clash-free manual gear changes by matching gear speeds before engagement.
- **Differential** allows the two wheels on an axle to rotate at **different speeds** (essential for cornering, since the outer wheel travels farther/faster than the inner wheel).
- **Universal (Hooke's) joints** on the propeller shaft accommodate the changing angle between gearbox output and rear axle, due to suspension movement.
