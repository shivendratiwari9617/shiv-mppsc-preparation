# Types of Boilers & Mountings (बॉयलर के प्रकार व माउंटिंग्स)

## Boiler — Basic Definition

A closed vessel in which **water is converted into steam** by application of heat (from burning fuel or another heat source), at a pressure higher than atmospheric — the steam produced is used for power generation (steam turbines), heating, or industrial process purposes.

## Classification of Boilers — Fire-Tube vs Water-Tube (extremely high-yield)

| | Fire-Tube Boiler | Water-Tube Boiler |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Arrangement | **Hot gases (fire) pass through tubes**, surrounded by **water** in an outer shell | **Water passes through tubes**, surrounded/heated by **hot gases (fire)** outside the tubes |
| Pressure capability | **Lower** pressure (typically up to ~17.5 bar / 250 psi) | **Higher** pressure (can go much higher, well beyond 100 bar in modern power-station boilers) |
| Steam generation rate/capacity | Lower | Higher |
| Response to load changes | **Slower** (large water volume, high thermal inertia) | **Faster** (less water volume, quicker steam response) |
| Risk in case of explosion | More **hazardous** (large water volume stores more explosive energy, catastrophic rupture risk) | Comparatively **safer** (smaller water volume per tube, failure is more localized/less catastrophic) |
| Examples | **Cochran boiler, Lancashire boiler, Cornish boiler, Locomotive boiler** | **Babcock and Wilcox boiler, Stirling boiler, La Mont boiler, Benson boiler** |

**Key memorized rule: Fire-tube = fire/gas INSIDE tubes, water OUTSIDE (in the shell); Water-tube = water INSIDE tubes, fire/gas OUTSIDE.** This single rule resolves nearly every classification question in this topic.

## Other Boiler Classifications

| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Axis of shell | **Horizontal, Vertical, Inclined** boiler |
| Position of furnace | **Internally fired** (furnace inside the shell, e.g., Cochran, Lancashire) / **Externally fired** (furnace outside the shell, e.g., Babcock and Wilcox) |
| Circulation of water | **Natural circulation** (density difference between hot/cold water drives flow) / **Forced circulation** (a pump forces water circulation — used in high-pressure boilers like La Mont, Benson) |
| Number of tubes | **Single-tube** (e.g., Cornish) / **Multi-tube** (e.g., Lancashire, Cochran, locomotive) |
| Use of steam pressure | **Low pressure** (up to ~15-17.5 bar) / **Medium pressure** (17.5-80 bar) / **High pressure** (above 80 bar) |

## Specific Named Boilers — Brief Facts

| Boiler | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Cochran Boiler** | Vertical, multi-tubular, fire-tube — compact, suited for small capacity requirements |
| **Lancashire Boiler** | Horizontal, fire-tube, has **two large fire tubes** running through the shell — widely used historically for medium-pressure industrial steam supply |
| **Cornish Boiler** | Horizontal, fire-tube, has only **one** large fire tube (simpler predecessor concept to Lancashire) |
| **Locomotive Boiler** | Horizontal, fire-tube, multi-tubular, **mobile** (fitted to steam locomotives), compact and high steam-generation rate for its size |
| **Babcock and Wilcox Boiler** | Water-tube, externally fired, widely used for **higher-pressure** power generation |

## Boiler Mountings vs Boiler Accessories — Critical Distinction (extremely high-yield)

| | Mountings | Accessories |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Fittings **essential for the safe operation and control** of the boiler itself | Fittings that **improve the efficiency/performance** of the boiler plant, but are not strictly essential to basic safe operation |
| Mandatory? | **Yes** — a boiler cannot legally/safely operate without its mountings | Not strictly mandatory for basic safe operation, though highly beneficial for efficiency |
| Mounted directly on the boiler shell? | **Yes**, directly on the boiler shell/drum | Often installed **separately** in the steam/feed-water/flue-gas circuit, not necessarily directly on the shell itself |
| Examples | **Safety valve, Pressure gauge, Water level indicator, Stop valve, Blow-off cock, Feed check valve, Fusible plug, Man-hole/Mud-hole** | **Economizer, Superheater, Feed pump, Air preheater, Steam separator** |

**Key memory rule: Mountings = SAFETY & CONTROL essentials, mandatory, on the boiler itself. Accessories = EFFICIENCY-improving equipment, in the surrounding steam circuit.**

## Boiler Mountings — Individual Functions (very high-yield)

| Mounting | Function |
|---|---|
| **Safety Valve** | Automatically releases excess steam if pressure exceeds the safe design limit, preventing explosion — the single most critical safety mounting |
| **Water Level Indicator (Gauge Glass)** | Shows the operator the current water level in the boiler — critical, since running with too little water risks the shell overheating/failing |
| **Pressure Gauge** | Indicates the steam pressure inside the boiler |
| **Fusible Plug** | A plug made of a low-melting-point alloy, located where it would be exposed if water level drops dangerously low — **melts** at that point, releasing pressure/extinguishing the fire, protecting against a dangerously low water level condition (an automatic, "fail-safe" safety device, distinct from the pressure-triggered safety valve) |
| **Stop Valve** | Controls (starts/stops) the flow of steam from the boiler to the steam pipeline/turbine |
| **Blow-off Cock** | Used to periodically drain sediment/mud/scale from the bottom of the boiler, and to fully empty the boiler when needed |
| **Feed Check Valve** | A one-way (non-return) valve controlling the entry of feed water into the boiler, preventing water from flowing backward out of the boiler |
| **Man-hole/Mud-hole** | Openings allowing personnel access for internal inspection/cleaning |

## Boiler Accessories — Individual Functions

| Accessory | Function |
|---|---|
| **Economizer** | Preheats feed water using the **waste heat of exhaust (flue) gases**, before it enters the boiler — improves overall thermal efficiency |
| **Superheater** | Further heats saturated steam (produced in the boiler) to a higher temperature, producing **superheated (dry) steam** — improves turbine efficiency and reduces moisture-related erosion damage in turbine blades |
| **Air Preheater** | Preheats the **combustion air** using waste flue-gas heat, before it enters the furnace — improves combustion efficiency |
| **Feed Pump** | Supplies (pumps) feed water into the boiler against the boiler's internal pressure |

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **Fire-tube boiler**: fire/gas inside tubes, water surrounds them in the shell — lower pressure, slower response, larger explosion hazard.
- **Water-tube boiler**: water inside tubes, fire/gas surrounds them — higher pressure, faster response, safer in case of failure.
- **Mountings** (safety valve, water level indicator, pressure gauge, fusible plug, stop valve, blow-off cock, feed check valve) are **mandatory safety/control** fittings, directly on the boiler.
- **Accessories** (economizer, superheater, air preheater, feed pump) **improve efficiency**, not strictly mandatory for basic safe operation.
- **Fusible plug** protects against **low water level** (melts and vents); **Safety valve** protects against **excess pressure** (releases steam) — two distinct safety mechanisms, frequently confused.

## Practice Questions

1. In a fire-tube boiler, does the hot gas flow inside the tubes or outside them (surrounded by water)? → **Inside the tubes** (water surrounds them in the shell)
2. Which boiler type is generally capable of higher pressure and faster response to load changes — fire-tube or water-tube? → **Water-tube boiler**
3. Which boiler mounting is designed to melt and release pressure/extinguish the fire specifically when the water level drops dangerously low? → **Fusible plug**
4. Is a superheater classified as a boiler mounting or a boiler accessory? → **Accessory** (improves efficiency/steam quality, not mandatory for basic safe operation)
