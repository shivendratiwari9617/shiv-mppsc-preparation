# Laws of Friction — Angle of Friction, Cone of Friction (घर्षण के नियम — घर्षण कोण, घर्षण शंकु)

## Friction — Basic Definition

**Friction** is the resistive force that opposes the relative motion (or tendency of relative motion) between two surfaces in contact.

## Types of Friction

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Static Friction** | Opposes the tendency of relative motion between surfaces at rest; varies from 0 up to a maximum limiting value |
| **Limiting Friction** | The maximum value of static friction, just before motion begins |
| **Dynamic/Kinetic Friction** | Friction acting when one surface is actually sliding over another; always ≤ limiting friction |
| **Rolling Friction** | Friction when one body rolls over another (e.g., ball/roller bearings) — much smaller than sliding friction |
| **Fluid Friction** | Resistance between layers of a moving fluid |

## Laws of Static Friction

1. Force of friction always acts in a direction **opposite** to the direction of motion (or tendency of motion).
2. Magnitude of friction is **exactly equal** to the force which tends to move the body, until the limiting value is reached.
3. Magnitude of limiting friction bears a **constant ratio** to the normal reaction between the two surfaces (this ratio is the coefficient of friction).
4. Force of friction is **independent of the area of contact**, as long as the normal reaction remains the same.
5. Force of friction depends upon the **roughness/smoothness** of the surfaces.

## Laws of Kinetic (Dynamic) Friction

1. Kinetic friction always acts opposite to the direction of relative motion.
2. It bears a constant ratio to the normal reaction, but this ratio is **slightly less** than the limiting static friction ratio.
3. For moderate speeds, kinetic friction remains **constant** but decreases slightly as speed increases.

## Coefficient of Friction (μ)

**μ = F / N**

where F = limiting friction force, N = normal reaction.

- μ is a **dimensionless** number, depends on the nature of the two surfaces in contact (material + roughness).
- **μs (static) is always ≥ μk (kinetic)** — this is why more force is needed to *start* motion than to *sustain* it.

## Angle of Friction (φ) — घर्षण कोण

The angle between the **normal reaction (N)** and the **resultant reaction (R)** of the normal reaction and the limiting friction force, at the point of impending motion.

**tan φ = F / N = μ**

So: **φ = tan⁻¹(μ)**

## Angle of Repose

The maximum angle of an inclined plane at which a body placed on it remains in equilibrium (just about to slide) purely under gravity and friction, without any external force.

**Angle of repose = Angle of friction (φ)** — numerically these two angles are equal, a frequently tested fact.

- On an incline of angle θ: if θ < φ (angle of friction), the body remains stationary; if θ > φ, the body slides down.

## Cone of Friction (घर्षण शंकु)

If the angle of friction φ is rotated through 360° about the normal, it generates an imaginary cone called the **cone of friction**. As long as the resultant reaction lies **within** this cone, the body remains in equilibrium (no slipping) — this is the 3D generalization of the angle of friction concept, used in analyzing motion on inclined planes and in screw-jack/machine problems.

## Ladder Friction (common application)

A classic problem type: a ladder resting against a wall and floor, with friction at both contact points — used to find the minimum angle at which the ladder will not slip, applying equilibrium equations along with friction limiting conditions at both supports.

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- **μ = tan φ** — coefficient of friction equals the tangent of the angle of friction. Extremely frequently asked.
- **Angle of repose = Angle of friction** numerically.
- Friction is **independent of contact area**, dependent on normal reaction and surface nature.
- **μs > μk** always (static friction coefficient is greater than kinetic).
- Rolling friction << Sliding friction << Static (limiting) friction, in general magnitude — this is why bearings/wheels reduce friction losses.
- Friction opposes relative motion — a body at rest experiences static friction only up to the point external force exceeds the limiting value.

## Practice Questions

1. If the coefficient of friction between a body and an incline is 0.577, what is the angle of friction? → tan⁻¹(0.577) ≈ **30°**
2. A block just starts sliding on an incline of 25°. What is the angle of repose? → **25°** (equal to incline angle at the point of sliding)
3. Does the force of friction depend on the area of contact? → **No**, it depends on normal reaction and surface roughness only.
