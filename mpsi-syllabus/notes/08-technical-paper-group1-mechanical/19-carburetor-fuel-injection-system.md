# Carburetor & Fuel Injection System (कार्बुरेटर व ईंधन इंजेक्शन प्रणाली)

## Carburetor (Petrol/SI Engines) — Basic Function

A device that **mixes air and fuel in the correct proportion** and delivers this atomized mixture to the engine cylinders, based on the principle of the **Venturi effect** (Bernoulli's principle — see point 21).

### Working Principle
Air flowing through a narrowed passage (**venturi/choke tube**) in the carburetor speeds up, causing a **local pressure drop**. This low pressure **draws (siphons) fuel** from the float chamber through a **fuel jet**, atomizing it into the airstream — the greater the airflow (throttle opening), the more fuel is drawn, roughly maintaining the correct air-fuel ratio.

### Main Parts of a Carburetor

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Float Chamber** | Maintains a constant fuel level, supplied via a float-and-needle-valve mechanism (like a cistern) |
| **Venturi (Choke Tube)** | Narrowed throat that accelerates airflow, creating the pressure drop needed to draw fuel |
| **Fuel Jet (Main Jet)** | Meters and delivers fuel into the venturi throat |
| **Throttle Valve (Butterfly Valve)** | Controls the quantity of air-fuel mixture entering the engine — this is what the accelerator pedal/throttle controls |
| **Choke Valve** | Restricts air intake to enrich the mixture for **cold starting** |
| **Idling/Slow-Running Jet** | Supplies fuel for smooth running at idle (throttle nearly closed, insufficient venturi vacuum for main jet) |

### Air-Fuel Ratio (A:F Ratio)

**Stoichiometric (chemically correct) A:F ratio for petrol ≈ 14.7 : 1 (by mass)** — the ratio at which all fuel is theoretically burnt completely with all the oxygen supplied, with nothing left over; used as the reference for "rich" (more fuel, A:F ratio lower than 14.7:1) and "lean" (less fuel, ratio higher) mixtures.

- **Rich mixture**: more fuel relative to air — needed for cold starting, high-load/acceleration.
- **Lean mixture**: less fuel relative to air — used for cruising/economy, but excessively lean mixtures can cause misfiring or overheating.

## Fuel Injection System — Overview

Delivers fuel directly under pressure through an **injector/nozzle**, rather than relying on a venturi's suction effect — used universally in modern petrol engines (replacing carburetors) and is the **only** method used in diesel/CI engines (since diesel engines induct only air, with no fuel present to create a venturi mixture).

### Petrol Injection Types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Throttle Body Injection (TBI)** | Single injector(s) located at the throttle body (where a carburetor would sit) — simple, early electronic fuel injection form |
| **Multi-Point Fuel Injection (MPFI)** | Separate injector for each cylinder, located near the intake port — more precise, better efficiency/performance, standard in modern petrol cars |
| **Gasoline Direct Injection (GDI)** | Fuel injected directly into the combustion chamber (not the intake port) at very high pressure — improves efficiency and power further |

### Diesel Fuel Injection System — Key Components

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| **Fuel Tank & Feed Pump** | Supplies diesel from tank to the injection pump at low pressure |
| **Fuel Filter** | Removes contaminants — critical since injector clearances are extremely small |
| **Fuel Injection Pump (FIP)** | Pressurizes fuel to very high pressure and delivers the precise metered quantity to each injector at the correct timing |
| **Injector (Nozzle)** | Atomizes and sprays high-pressure fuel directly into the combustion chamber near the end of the compression stroke |
| **Governor** | Regulates fuel quantity to control engine speed (see also point 28) |

### Types of Diesel Injection Systems

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **In-line (Jerk) Pump** | One pumping element per cylinder, all driven by a common camshaft — traditional mechanical system |
| **Distributor (Rotary) Pump** | Single pumping element distributes fuel to all cylinders in sequence via a rotating distributor |
| **Common Rail Direct Injection (CRDI)** | Fuel is pressurized once into a common high-pressure "rail," from which electronically controlled injectors draw fuel independently for each cylinder — allows very precise, multiple injections per cycle, used in virtually all modern diesel engines (better efficiency, lower emissions, less noise) |
| **Unit Injector** | Pump and injector combined into a single unit per cylinder, mounted directly in the cylinder head |

## Carburetor vs Fuel Injection — Comparison

| | Carburetor | Fuel Injection |
|---|---|---|
| Mixture control | Mechanical, venturi-based (less precise) | Electronically/precisely metered (more precise) |
| Fuel efficiency | Lower | Higher |
| Emissions | Higher | Lower |
| Cold starting | Needs choke | Easier (electronic control adapts automatically) |
| Modern usage | Largely obsolete in cars; still found in small engines (older two-wheelers, small generators) | Standard in virtually all modern petrol and diesel vehicles |

## Most Important Facts to Remember

- Carburetor works on the **venturi effect** (Bernoulli's principle) — airflow speeds up through a narrow throat, pressure drops, fuel is drawn in.
- **Stoichiometric air-fuel ratio for petrol ≈ 14.7 : 1** (by mass) — a specific number worth memorizing.
- **Choke valve** = for cold starting (enriches mixture); **Throttle valve** = controls overall mixture quantity (power output).
- Diesel engines **never** use a carburetor — fuel is always injected directly under high pressure since only air is inducted through the intake.
- **CRDI (Common Rail Direct Injection)** is the modern standard diesel injection system — precise electronic control, multiple injections per cycle, high pressure common rail.
- MPFI (multi-point, one injector per cylinder) has largely replaced carburetors and throttle-body injection in modern petrol vehicles.

## Practice Questions

1. On what physical principle does a carburetor's fuel-drawing mechanism work? → **Venturi effect (Bernoulli's principle) — pressure drop due to accelerated airflow**
2. What is the approximate stoichiometric air-fuel ratio for petrol, by mass? → **14.7 : 1**
3. Why does a diesel engine never use a carburetor? → **Because only air is inducted through the intake; fuel is injected separately and directly, so there's no fuel present to be drawn via venturi suction**
4. What does "CRDI" stand for in modern diesel engines? → **Common Rail Direct Injection**
