# Subject-Verb Agreement Rules

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar Fundamentals | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — subject-verb agreement is one of the top 3 most tested error-spotting/fill-in-the-blank categories in nearly every English paper.

---

## 1. The Basic Rule

A verb must agree with its subject in **number** (singular/plural) and **person**.

"He **writes**." (singular) / "They **write**." (plural)

Beyond this basic rule, there are several specific sub-rules that account for almost all the tricky agreement questions asked in competitive exams.

---

## 2. The 13 Key Agreement Rules

| # | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Singular subject → singular verb; plural subject → plural verb | The boy plays. / The boys play. |
| 2 | Subjects joined by "and" usually take a plural verb | Ram and Shyam **are** friends. |
| 2a | **Exception:** if "and" joins two parts referring to one person/idea, use singular | Bread and butter **is** my favourite breakfast. |
| 3 | With "or / nor / either...or / neither...nor", the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. |
| 4 | Collective nouns: singular verb when acting as one unit, plural when members act individually | The team **is** playing well. / The team **are** quarrelling among themselves. |
| 5 | Indefinite pronouns (each, everyone, everybody, someone, anybody, nobody, no one) always take a **singular** verb | Everyone **is** happy. |
| 6 | Uncountable nouns (news, information, furniture, advice, luggage) take a **singular** verb even if they look plural | The news **is** shocking. |
| 7 | Nouns that look plural but are singular in meaning (mathematics, physics, news, measles) take a **singular** verb | Mathematics **is** my favourite subject. |
| 8 | Nouns that look singular but are always plural (scissors, trousers, spectacles, pants) take a **plural** verb | The scissors **are** on the table. |
| 9 | "There is/are" — the verb agrees with the noun that **follows** it | There **is** a book on the table. / There **are** many books on the table. |
| 10 | Phrases like "along with / as well as / together with / in addition to / besides" do **not** change the verb — agree with the main subject only | The teacher, along with the students, **is** going on a trip. |
| 11 | Fractions/percentages — verb agrees with the noun that follows "of" | Half of the cake **is** eaten. / Half of the students **are** absent. |
| 12 | A single quantity of money, distance, or time is treated as **singular** | Ten thousand rupees **is** a huge amount. |
| 13 | "Each" or "every" before subjects joined by "and" — verb stays **singular** | Every boy and every girl **was** present. |

---

## 3. Most-Confused Rules (Special Attention)

- **Rule 3 vs Rule 2:** "and" almost always → plural verb; "or/nor" → verb follows the **nearer** subject only. These are the two most frequently mixed-up rules.
- **Rule 6/7 vs Rule 8:** Some plural-looking nouns are singular (news, mathematics); some singular-looking nouns are always plural (scissors, trousers) — memorize both lists, don't rely on "looks."
- **Rule 10** is a classic trap: exam-writers deliberately insert a plural noun right before the verb via "along with/as well as" to tempt you into a plural verb — always trace back to the **true subject**.

## 4. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Sentence | Correct verb | Rule applied |
|---|---|---|
| "Neither he nor his friends ___ coming." | are | Rule 3 (nearer subject: friends) |
| "The furniture in this house ___ old." | is | Rule 6 (uncountable noun) |
| "The committee ___ divided in its opinion." | is (or "are," if members act individually) | Rule 4 |
| "Each of the boys ___ a bicycle." | has | Rule 5 |
| "The scissors ___ sharp." | are | Rule 8 |
| "The captain, along with his team, ___ arrived." | has | Rule 10 |

---

## 5. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. Rules 2, 3, and 10 (and / or-nor / along-with) form the **single biggest cluster** of agreement questions — master these three first.
2. Keep a fixed list of "always singular despite looking plural" (news, mathematics, physics, measles) and "always plural despite looking singular" (scissors, trousers, spectacles) — no rule predicts these, they must be memorized directly.
3. When checking agreement in a long sentence, **mentally strip out** parenthetical phrases (along with..., as well as..., in addition to...) to expose the real subject before deciding the verb.
4. This topic is a direct prerequisite for point 14 (Spotting errors) and point 15 (Sentence correction) — agreement errors are among the most common error types planted in those question types.
