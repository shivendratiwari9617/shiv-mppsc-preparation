# Idioms and Phrases — Common Usage and Meaning

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Vocabulary | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — this parallels Hindi point 10 (मुहावरे व लोकोक्तियाँ); English idiom-meaning questions are equally common.

---

## 1. What is an Idiom?

A phrase whose meaning **cannot be understood from the literal meaning of its individual words** — its meaning is figurative and fixed by common usage.

## 3. Strategy for Idiom Questions

1. **Never translate literally** — the whole point of an idiom is that its literal reading is wrong.
2. When the exact idiom is unfamiliar, use the **surrounding sentence context** to infer whether the tone is positive, negative, or neutral, and match the closest-sounding option.
3. Many idioms cluster around a theme (weather: "under the weather," "rain cats and dogs"; body parts: "cost an arm and a leg," "pull someone's leg") — grouping by theme aids memorization, same as the Hindi मुहावरे strategy (Hindi point 10).

## 5. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. This is a **pure memorization topic** — there is no grammatical rule to derive an idiom's meaning, so repeated exposure to the list (Section 2) is the only reliable strategy.
2. Idiom questions are usually asked in **two directions**: (a) idiom given, choose the meaning; (b) meaning/scenario given, choose the matching idiom — practice both directions.
3. Don't confuse idioms with **literal figurative language** in comprehension passages (point 19) — idioms are fixed expressions, not spontaneous metaphors.
4. This directly parallels Hindi point 10 — the underlying test-taking skill (recognize fixed expression → recall memorized meaning) is identical in both languages.
