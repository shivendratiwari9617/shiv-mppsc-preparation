# Active and Passive Voice Conversion

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar / Sentence Transformation | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — voice conversion is a near-guaranteed question type; it directly parallels the Hindi वाच्य-परिवर्तन topic (point 27 in the Hindi note series).

---

## 1. Active vs Passive Voice

| Voice | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Subject **performs** the action | Ram wrote a letter. |
| **Passive** | Subject **receives** the action | A letter was written by Ram. |

### Basic Conversion Rule
1. **Object** of the active sentence becomes the **Subject** of the passive sentence.
2. **Verb** becomes **"be" (in the correct tense/form) + V3 (past participle)**.
3. **Subject** of the active sentence becomes **"by + subject"** at the end (often omitted if not important).

---

## 2. Tense-Wise Passive Voice Structure (High-Yield Table)

| Tense | Active structure | Passive structure |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | S + V1 + O | O + is/am/are + V3 + by + S |
| Present Continuous | S + is/am/are + V-ing + O | O + is/am/are + being + V3 + by + S |
| Present Perfect | S + has/have + V3 + O | O + has/have + been + V3 + by + S |
| Simple Past | S + V2 + O | O + was/were + V3 + by + S |
| Past Continuous | S + was/were + V-ing + O | O + was/were + being + V3 + by + S |
| Past Perfect | S + had + V3 + O | O + had + been + V3 + by + S |
| Simple Future | S + will + V1 + O | O + will be + V3 + by + S |
| Future Perfect | S + will have + V3 + O | O + will have been + V3 + by + S |
| Modals (can/could/may/must etc.) | S + modal + V1 + O | O + modal + be + V3 + by + S |

**Note:** Present Perfect Continuous, Past Perfect Continuous, Future Continuous, and Future Perfect Continuous are **not normally converted** into passive voice — this itself is a commonly tested fact.

---

## 3. Special Conversion Rules

### (a) Sentences with two objects (indirect + direct object)
Can form **two possible passive sentences**:
"He gave me a book." → "I was given a book by him." **OR** "A book was given to me by him."

### (b) Imperative sentences (commands/requests)
| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Open the door. (command) | Let the door be opened. |
| Please help me. (request) | You are requested to help me. |

### (c) Interrogative sentences
| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Did he write the letter? (Yes/No question) | Was the letter written by him? |
| Who wrote this letter? (Wh-question) | By whom was this letter written? |

### (d) Dropping "by + agent"
When the doer is obvious, unknown, or unimportant, "by + agent" is dropped:
"They speak English here." → "English is spoken here."

### (e) Preposition changes with certain verbs
Some verbs require a specific preposition in the passive form:
| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| The news surprised me. | I was surprised **at** the news. |
| This pleased her. | She was pleased **with** this. |
| This satisfies me. | I am satisfied **with** this. |

---

## 4. Verbs That Cannot Normally Be Used in Passive Voice

Intransitive verbs (no object) and certain stative verbs are **not converted** to passive: sleep, die, arrive, happen, occur, resemble, have (in the sense of possession), fit, suit, become.

"He resembles his father." — **cannot** be converted to passive (no valid passive form exists for this usage).

---

## 5. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| She is writing a letter. | A letter is being written by her. |
| He has completed the work. | The work has been completed by him. |
| They will finish the project by 5 PM. | The project will be finished by them by 5 PM. |
| Can you solve this problem? | Can this problem be solved by you? |
| Close the window. | Let the window be closed. |
| Who broke the window? | By whom was the window broken? |

---

## 6. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. Master the **12 tense-to-passive mappings (Section 2)** — this is the backbone of every voice-conversion question.
2. Remember which tenses **cannot** be converted to passive (perfect continuous forms, future continuous) — this fact itself is directly tested.
3. **Imperative and interrogative sentence conversion (Section 3b, 3c)** follow different rules from statements — don't force the standard S-V-O pattern onto them.
4. Some verbs (intransitive/stative — Section 4) have **no valid passive form** — recognizing these avoids a common trap where a "correct-looking" passive sentence is actually ungrammatical.
5. This topic maps directly to Hindi point 27 (वाच्य परिवर्तन) — the underlying logic (object becomes subject, verb form changes) is the same in both languages, which helps cross-reinforce both.
