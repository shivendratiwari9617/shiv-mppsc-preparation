# Cloze Test (Paragraph with Blanks)

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar & Vocabulary Application | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — a cloze test is essentially a **paragraph-length version of point 16 (Fill in the blanks)**, where every blank must also make sense with the surrounding blanks and the paragraph's overall logic.

---

## 1. What is a Cloze Test?

A **paragraph** with several words removed and replaced with numbered blanks; for each blank, you choose the correct word from given options. Unlike a single-sentence fill-in-the-blank, every choice must fit **the paragraph as a whole**, not just its own sentence.

---

## 2. How Cloze Tests Differ from Single-Blank Questions

| Single-blank (point 16) | Cloze test |
|---|---|
| Context is one sentence | Context is an entire paragraph |
| Blanks are independent | Blanks are often **interdependent** — an earlier blank's answer can affect a later one's logic |
| Mostly grammar/vocabulary | Also tests **paragraph coherence** — tense consistency, logical connectors, tone consistency across the whole passage |

---

## 3. Strategy — Step by Step

1. **Read the entire paragraph first, blanks and all**, to grasp the overall topic and tone before answering anything.
2. Identify the **tense being used throughout** — cloze paragraphs are usually written in one consistent tense (or a clearly logical shift), and answers must match it (point 2 — Tenses).
3. For each blank, check what **part of speech** is required — noun, verb, adjective, preposition, or connector (point 1 — Parts of speech).
4. Watch for **connector words** (however, therefore, moreover, although) that signal the logical relationship between sentences — these often anchor whole-paragraph coherence (point 8 — Conjunctions and connectors).
5. After filling all blanks, **re-read the completed paragraph** to confirm it reads naturally and consistently — this final check catches most remaining errors.

---

## 4. Common Blank Categories in Cloze Tests

| Category | What's tested |
|---|---|
| Verb form/tense | Correct tense matching the paragraph's established time frame |
| Preposition | Fixed verb/adjective + preposition pairs (point 7) |
| Article | a/an/the/zero article (point 6) |
| Connector | Logical relationship between sentences (point 8) |
| Vocabulary | Word choice based on tone/context, including collocations (point 16) |

---

## 5. Worked Example

> "Ravi had always wanted to become a doctor. Since childhood, he ___(1)___ books on medicine and biology. His parents, ___(2)___ were both teachers, encouraged his interest. ___(3)___, when the time came for him to choose a career, he decided to study engineering instead — a decision that surprised everyone ___(4)___ knew him."

| Blank | Options (typical) | Correct | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1) | reads / had read / was reading | had read | Past perfect — action completed before another past reference ("since childhood" leading up to a later point) |
| (2) | which / who / whom | who | Refers to people (his parents), subject of the relative clause |
| (3) | Moreover / However / Therefore | However | Signals contrast — his interest in medicine vs. his actual choice of engineering |
| (4) | who / which / whom | who | Refers to people ("everyone" as subject of "knew") |

---

## 6. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. **Never answer a blank in isolation** — always check it against the paragraph's established tense and logical flow, which is the core skill cloze tests are designed to test.
2. **Connector words (Section 4)** are frequently the deciding factor between otherwise-similar options — a wrong connector breaks the paragraph's logic even if the word itself is grammatically valid.
3. This topic is a direct combined application of points 1, 2, 6, 7, and 8 — strengthening those individually is the most efficient way to improve at cloze tests.
4. Always do a **final full re-read** after filling every blank — some errors are only visible when the whole paragraph is read together, not blank-by-blank.
