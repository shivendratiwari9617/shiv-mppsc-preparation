# Sentence Improvement / Sentence Correction

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar Application (Skill-Based) | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — closely related to point 14 (Spotting errors), but this format asks you to actively **select the best replacement** for an underlined portion, not just flag that an error exists.

---

## 1. Format and Difference from Point 14

A sentence has a portion **underlined**; you choose the best replacement from given options (or "No improvement" if the original is already best).

| Point 14 (Spotting errors) | Point 15 (Sentence improvement) |
|---|---|
| Identify **which segment** has an error | Identify **the best correction** for a given segment |
| The sentence may be entirely correct ("No error") | Even a grammatically valid sentence may need improving for clarity/concision/idiom |

---

## 2. Category 1 — Grammatical Correction

Apply the **same checks as point 14**: subject-verb agreement, tense, articles, prepositions, pronoun case, redundant conjunctions, modals. Always run this check first.

---

## 3. Category 2 — Conciseness (Reducing Wordiness)

| Wordy | Improved |
|---|---|
| Due to the fact that he was late, he missed the train. | Because he was late, he missed the train. |
| In spite of the fact that she was tired, she completed the work. | Although she was tired, she completed the work. |
| He is a man who is honest. | He is an honest man. |

---

## 4. Category 3 — Idiomatic Correctness

A sentence can be grammatically parseable yet **not idiomatic** — native usage requires specific fixed pairings, especially with comparison words.

| Non-idiomatic | Idiomatic |
|---|---|
| He is senior **than** me. | He is senior **to** me. |
| This is different **than** that. (standard British/Indian usage) | This is different **from** that. |
| He is junior **than** her. | He is junior **to** her. |

**Fixed comparison words that take "to," not "than":** senior to, junior to, prior to, superior to, inferior to, preferable to.

---

## 5. Category 4 — Clarity: Fixing Dangling/Misplaced Modifiers

A modifier (usually a participial phrase) must clearly refer to the correct noun — otherwise the sentence becomes illogical or ambiguous.

| Unclear (dangling modifier) | Clear |
|---|---|
| Walking down the street, the trees were beautiful. (trees can't walk) | Walking down the street, I found the trees beautiful. |
| Having finished the homework, the TV was turned on. (TV didn't finish homework) | Having finished the homework, he turned on the TV. |

| Ambiguous (misplaced modifier) | Clear |
|---|---|
| She almost drove her kids to school every day. (ambiguous — almost *drove*, or almost *every day*?) | She drove her kids to school almost every day. |

---

## 6. Strategy — Step-by-Step

1. Read the **whole sentence** first to understand the intended meaning, not just the underlined part.
2. Check the underlined portion against **all grammar rules** (Section 2 / point 14).
3. If grammatically fine, check for **conciseness, idiom, and clarity** issues (Sections 3-5).
4. Compare candidate replacement options and **eliminate any that introduce a new error**.
5. **"No improvement"** is a valid and sometimes correct answer — don't force a change onto an already-correct sentence.

---

## 7. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Original (underlined portion) | Best replacement |
|---|---|
| "He is superior **than** me in chess." | superior **to** |
| "Due to the fact that it rained, the match was cancelled." | Because it rained |
| "Running quickly, the bus was caught by him." | Running quickly, **he caught the bus.** |
| "She has more better handwriting than her sister." | better handwriting |

---

## 8. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. Always check grammar first (point 14's checklist), then move to **idiom and conciseness** — many test-takers stop at "is it grammatically valid?" and miss idiomatic-correctness errors like "senior than."
2. Memorize the **fixed "to" comparison words** (Section 4) — these appear repeatedly and are easy points once learned.
3. **Dangling modifiers (Section 5)** are subtle — the fix almost always requires identifying who/what is actually performing the introductory action and making sure the main clause's subject matches it.
4. Don't over-edit — if an option merely rephrases without fixing an actual problem, and the original had none, choose **"No improvement."**
