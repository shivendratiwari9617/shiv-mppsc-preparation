# Punctuation Rules

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar / Writing | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — parallels Hindi point 19 (विराम चिह्न); punctuation errors are a frequent error-spotting (point 14) category, especially the comma splice and apostrophe misuse.

---

## 1. Full Stop / Period ( . )

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| End of a declarative/imperative sentence | She went home. |
| Abbreviations | Dr., Mr., etc. |

---

## 2. Comma ( , )

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Separating items in a list | I bought apples, bananas, and oranges. |
| Before a coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses | He was tired, but he kept working. |
| After an introductory phrase/clause | After the meeting, we went home. |
| Setting off non-essential/parenthetical information | My brother, who lives in Delhi, is visiting. |

**Note (Oxford comma):** the comma before "and" in a list ("apples, bananas, and oranges") is expected in formal writing, though some styles omit it.

---

## 3. Semicolon ( ; )

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Joining two closely related independent clauses **without a conjunction** | She was late; the train was delayed. |
| Separating list items that themselves contain commas | We visited Delhi, India; Paris, France; and Tokyo, Japan. |

---

## 4. Colon ( : )

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Introducing a list | Bring these items: pen, notebook, and ruler. |
| Introducing an explanation/elaboration | He had one goal: to win. |
| Before a formal quotation | The report concluded: "Further study is needed." |

---

## 5. Apostrophe ( ' )

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Possession (singular) | Ram's book |
| Possession (plural ending in "s") | the students' books |
| Contractions | don't, it's (= it is) |

**Common error:** apostrophes are **never** used to form a simple plural — "apple's for sale" is wrong; it should be "apples for sale." This is one of the most frequently planted errors in objective papers.

---

## 6. Quotation Marks ( " " )

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Direct speech | He said, "I am coming." |
| Titles of short works (articles, poems) | She read "The Road Not Taken." |

---

## 7. Question Mark ( ? ) and Exclamation Mark ( ! )

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ? | End of a **direct** question | Where are you going? |
| ? | **Not** used for indirect/reported questions | He asked where I was going. (no "?") |
| ! | Expresses strong emotion | What a beautiful day! |

---

## 8. Hyphen ( - ) and Dash ( — )

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hyphen (-) | Joining compound words | mother-in-law, well-known |
| Hyphen (-) | Joining prefix to root to avoid confusion | re-enter (vs "reenter"), co-worker |
| Dash (—) | Setting off a strong interruption or emphasis | She was going to say something—but stopped. |

---

## 9. Parentheses ( )

Adding extra/supplementary information not essential to the main sentence: "The meeting (which was postponed twice) finally happened."

---

## 10. The Comma Splice — the #1 Punctuation Error in Objective Papers

A **comma splice** is joining two independent clauses with **only a comma**, with no conjunction — this is grammatically incorrect.

| Wrong (comma splice) | Correct options |
|---|---|
| She was tired, she went to bed. | She was tired, **so** she went to bed. (add conjunction) |
| | She was tired**;** she went to bed. (use a semicolon) |
| | She was tired. She went to bed. (use two sentences) |

---

## 11. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Sentence | Error type |
|---|---|
| "The bananas' are ripe." | Apostrophe misused for a simple plural |
| "It was raining, we stayed indoors." | Comma splice |
| "He asked, where I was going?" | Question mark wrongly used with reported speech |
| "My sister who lives in Pune is a doctor." (sister is an only sibling) | Missing commas around non-essential clause |

---

## 12. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. The **comma splice (Section 10)** is the single most repeated punctuation error in error-spotting questions — always check whether two independent clauses are joined by a bare comma.
2. **Apostrophe misuse for plurals** (Section 5) is the second most common trap — apostrophes mark possession or contraction, never a plain plural.
3. **Question marks with reported/indirect questions** (Section 7) is a subtle but recurring error — once a question becomes reported speech (point 5), it loses its question mark.
4. This topic parallels Hindi point 19 (विराम चिह्न) directly — both languages test whether you know which mark signals which kind of pause or relationship.
