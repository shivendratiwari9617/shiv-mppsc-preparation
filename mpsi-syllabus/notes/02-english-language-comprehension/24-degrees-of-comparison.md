# Degrees of Comparison (Positive/Comparative/Superlative)

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar Fundamentals | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — parallels Hindi point 7 (विशेषण — तुलना); irregular comparison forms and double-comparative errors are frequently tested.

---

## 1. The 3 Degrees

| Degree | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | Simple quality, no comparison | tall |
| Comparative | Comparing **two** things/people | taller |
| Superlative | Comparing **more than two** (the highest/lowest degree) | tallest |

---

## 2. Rules for Forming Comparative and Superlative

| Adjective type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| One-syllable | add -er/-est | tall → taller → tallest |
| One-syllable, short vowel + single consonant | double the consonant | big → bigger → biggest |
| Ending in silent "e" | drop the "e", add -er/-est | large → larger → largest |
| Two-syllable ending in "-y" | change "y" to "i", add -er/-est | happy → happier → happiest |
| Two-syllable (other) and longer (3+ syllables) | use "more"/"most" before the adjective | beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful |

---

## 3. Irregular Comparison Forms (High-Yield — Must Memorize)

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| little (quantity) | less | least |
| much/many | more | most |
| far (physical distance) | farther | farthest |
| far (figurative/additional) | further | furthest |
| old (general) | older | oldest |
| old (family seniority) | elder | eldest |

**Note:** "elder/eldest" is used **only** for family relationships (elder brother), never for general age comparisons of things/unrelated people ("older building," not "elder building").

---

## 4. Usage Rules

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Comparative is followed by "than" | She is taller **than** her sister. |
| Superlative is preceded by "the" and followed by "in/of" | She is **the** tallest girl **in** the class. |
| When comparing exactly **two** things, always use comparative, never superlative | He is **the taller** of the two brothers. (not "tallest") |
| Equal comparison uses "as...as" | She is as tall as her brother. |
| Negative comparison uses "not as/so...as" | He is not as tall as his friend. |

---

## 5. Common Errors — Double Comparatives/Superlatives (Cross-Reference Point 14)

Never combine "-er/-est" with "more/most" on the same word.

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| He is **more taller** than me. | He is taller than me. |
| This is the **most best** option. | This is the best option. |

---

## 6. Special Comparison Words Requiring "to," Not "than" (Cross-Reference Point 15)

Certain Latin-origin comparison adjectives are followed by **"to"**, not "than", as a fixed idiomatic rule.

| Word | Correct usage |
|---|---|
| senior | senior **to** |
| junior | junior **to** |
| superior | superior **to** |
| inferior | inferior **to** |
| prior | prior **to** |

"He is senior to me" (correct) — not "senior than me."

---

## 7. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Comparative of "good" | better |
| Superlative of "far" (physical distance) | farthest |
| "He is the ___ of the two players." (comparing 2) | better (not "best") |
| Correct: "He is senior ___ me." | to (not "than") |
| Fix: "This is the most best solution." | This is the best solution. |
| Comparative of "happy" | happier |

---

## 8. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. **Irregular forms (Section 3)** — good/better/best, bad/worse/worst, far/farther-further — are the highest-yield memorization item in this topic.
2. The **"comparing two → comparative, not superlative"** rule (Section 4) is a subtle but frequently tested distinction — "the taller of the two," never "the tallest of the two."
3. **Double comparatives/superlatives (Section 5)** are a classic error-spotting trap and directly reinforce point 14.
4. The **"senior to / junior to" fixed-preposition rule (Section 6)** ties directly to point 15 (Sentence improvement) — this exact error appears repeatedly across state exam papers.
