# Direct and Indirect (Reported) Speech

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar / Sentence Transformation | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — narration/speech conversion is one of the most rule-heavy and frequently tested topics; it parallels Hindi's प्रत्यक्ष-अप्रत्यक्ष कथन (Hindi point 27) but with a much richer tense-backshift system.

---

## 1. Direct vs Indirect Speech

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Speech** | The speaker's exact words, in quotation marks | He said, "I am going to school." |
| **Indirect/Reported Speech** | Reporting what was said, without quotation marks | He said that he was going to school. |

---

## 2. General Conversion Steps

1. Remove quotation marks.
2. Add a connector: **"that"** for statements, **"if/whether"** for yes/no questions, the **Wh-word** for Wh-questions, **"to"** for commands.
3. Change **pronouns** according to the subject/object of the reporting verb.
4. **Backshift the tense** (if the reporting verb is in the past tense).
5. Change **time/place expressions** to match the reporting context.

---

## 3. Tense Backshift Table (Direct → Indirect) — Core of This Topic

| Direct (original tense) | Indirect (backshifted tense) |
|---|---|
| Simple Present | Simple Past |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect |
| Present Perfect Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous |
| Simple Past | Past Perfect |
| Past Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous |
| Past Perfect | Past Perfect (no further change) |
| will | would |
| will be (Future Continuous) | would be |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must | had to (obligation) / must (if still true) |
| shall (offer) | should |

**Exception — no tense change:** If the reported sentence states a **universal truth, habitual fact, or the reporting verb is in the present tense**, the tense does **not** shift.

"He said, 'The sun rises in the east.'" → "He said that the sun **rises** in the east." (not "rose")

---

## 4. Pronoun Change Rules

| Pronoun in direct speech | Changes according to |
|---|---|
| 1st person (I, we, my, our) | The **subject** of the reporting verb |
| 2nd person (you, your) | The **object** of the reporting verb |
| 3rd person (he, she, it, they) | Usually **unchanged** |

---

## 5. Time and Place Expression Changes

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| here | there |
| ago | before |

---

## 6. Rules by Sentence Type (Most Important Section)

### (a) Assertive/Statement sentences
Reporting verb "say" stays "say/said"; if there's an object ("said **to me**"), it changes to "tell/told".

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "I am happy." | He said that he was happy. |
| He said to me, "I am happy." | He told me that he was happy. |

### (b) Interrogative sentences (Questions)
**Yes/No questions:** reporting verb → "asked/inquired/wanted to know"; add **"if/whether"**; word order reverts to statement order (no auxiliary inversion); remove question mark.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "Are you coming?" | He asked if/whether I was coming. |

**Wh-questions:** reporting verb → "asked"; the **Wh-word itself** acts as the connector (no "that"/"if"); word order reverts to statement order.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "Where are you going?" | He asked where I was going. |

### (c) Imperative sentences (Commands/Requests)
Reporting verb → "ordered/told/requested/advised" (depending on tone); use **"to" + base verb**; no "that".

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "Go away!" | He ordered (me) to go away. |
| He said, "Please help me." | He requested me to help him. |
| He said, "Don't go there." | He told me not to go there. (negative: "not to") |

### (d) Exclamatory sentences
Reporting verb → "exclaimed with joy/sorrow/wonder"; remove exclamation mark; convert to statement structure with "that".

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "Hurrah! We have won." | He exclaimed with joy that they had won. |
| She said, "Alas! I have failed." | She exclaimed with sorrow that she had failed. |

### (e) Optative sentences (Wishes/Blessings)
| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| He said, "May you live long!" | He wished that I might live long. |

## 7. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| She said, "I am reading a book." | She said that she was reading a book. |
| He said to me, "Will you help me?" | He asked me if/whether I would help him. |
| The teacher said, "Sit down, students." | The teacher told/ordered the students to sit down. |
| She said, "What is your name?" | She asked what my name was. |
| He said, "I have finished my work." | He said that he had finished his work. |

---

## 8. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. The **tense backshift table (Section 3)** is the single highest-yield element — nearly every conversion question tests whether you shift the tense correctly.
2. **Sentence-type rules (Section 6)** determine which connector and reporting verb to use — statements ("that"), yes/no questions ("if/whether"), Wh-questions (the Wh-word itself), commands ("to"/"not to"). Mixing these up is the most common error.
3. Remember the **exception for universal truths** (Section 3) — this is a frequently planted trap where test-takers wrongly backshift a fact that should stay in present tense.
4. This topic parallels Hindi point 27 (वाच्य परिवर्तन एवं वाक्य परिवर्तन) — the underlying logic of pronoun/time-word shifting is conceptually the same in both languages, even though English's tense-backshift system is more elaborate.
