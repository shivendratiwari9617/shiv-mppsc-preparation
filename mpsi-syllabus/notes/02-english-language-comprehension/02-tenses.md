# Tenses — Present/Past/Future, Simple/Continuous/Perfect/Perfect-Continuous

**Subject:** English Language Comprehension | **Section:** Grammar Fundamentals | **Exam relevance:** MPSI along with MPPSC, Vyapam/ESB, Patwari, MP Police — the 12-tense system is the single most heavily tested English grammar topic across almost every state exam, and feeds directly into error-spotting, sentence correction, and fill-in-the-blanks.

---

## 1. What is Tense?

**Tense** indicates **when** an action happens (Present, Past, Future) and **how** it happens — its aspect (Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous). 3 times × 4 aspects = **12 tenses**.

---

## 2. The 12 Tenses — Structure, Example, Usage

### Present Tenses
| Tense | Structure | Example | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | Subject + V1(s/es) | She writes a letter. | Habits, facts, general truths |
| Present Continuous | Subject + is/am/are + V-ing | She is writing a letter. | Action happening right now |
| Present Perfect | Subject + has/have + V3 | She has written a letter. | Recently completed action, present relevance |
| Present Perfect Continuous | Subject + has/have + been + V-ing | She has been writing since morning. | Started in past, still continuing |

### Past Tenses
| Tense | Structure | Example | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Past | Subject + V2 | She wrote a letter. | Completed action in the past |
| Past Continuous | Subject + was/were + V-ing | She was writing a letter. | Ongoing action in the past |
| Past Perfect | Subject + had + V3 | She had written the letter before he arrived. | Action completed before another past action |
| Past Perfect Continuous | Subject + had + been + V-ing | She had been writing for an hour before he arrived. | Ongoing action up to another past point |

### Future Tenses
| Tense | Structure | Example | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple Future | Subject + will/shall + V1 | She will write a letter. | Future action |
| Future Continuous | Subject + will be + V-ing | She will be writing at 5 PM. | Ongoing action at a future point |
| Future Perfect | Subject + will have + V3 | She will have written the letter by 5 PM. | Action completed before a future point |
| Future Perfect Continuous | Subject + will have been + V-ing | She will have been writing for an hour by then. | Ongoing action up to a future point |

## 3. Signal Words (Very High-Yield for Tense Identification)

| Tense | Common signal words |
|---|---|
| Simple Present | always, usually, often, every day, generally |
| Present Continuous | now, right now, at the moment, currently |
| Present Perfect | already, just, yet, since, for, ever, never |
| Present Perfect Continuous | since, for, all day, lately |
| Simple Past | yesterday, ago, last week, in [past year] |
| Past Continuous | while, when, as (interrupted ongoing action) |
| Past Perfect | before, after, already (by a past point), had |
| Past Perfect Continuous | since, for + a past reference point |
| Simple Future | tomorrow, next week, will, shall |
| Future Continuous | at this time tomorrow, at [time] tomorrow |
| Future Perfect | by [time], by the time |
| Future Perfect Continuous | by [time] + for [duration] |

## 4. Common Errors — High-Yield for MPSI-Style Papers

| Wrong | Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| I have seen him yesterday. | I saw him yesterday. | A **specific past time** word (yesterday) requires Simple Past, not Present Perfect. |
| I am knowing the answer. | I know the answer. | Stative verbs (know, believe, own, love) are not normally used in continuous form. |
| She is living here since 2010. | She has been living here since 2010. | "Since" with an ongoing action needs Present Perfect Continuous, not Present Continuous. |
| He has gone there since Monday. | He has been there since Monday. | "Since" a point in time pairs with Perfect (Continuous) forms — check duration vs completion. |

### Since vs For
| Word | Used with |
|---|---|
| **since** | a specific point in time — since 2010, since Monday, since morning |
| **for** | a duration — for two years, for a week, for an hour |

## 5. Practice — High-Probability Self-Test

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "She ___ (write) a letter right now." | is writing (Present Continuous) |
| "He ___ (finish) his homework before dinner." (past) | had finished (Past Perfect) |
| "They ___ (live) here for ten years." (still living) | have been living (Present Perfect Continuous) |
| "I ___ (see) him yesterday." | saw (Simple Past — not "have seen") |
| Identify signal word type: "by 5 PM" | Future Perfect |

---

## 6. Most Important Exam-Focused Points

1. Memorize the **12-tense structure table (Section 2)** cold — every tense-based fill-in-the-blank or error-spotting question reduces to recognizing structure + signal word.
2. **Signal words (Section 3)** are the fastest way to identify the correct tense in a blank — train yourself to spot them first.
3. The **Present Perfect + specific past-time word** error (Section 4) is the single most repeated trap in objective English papers — check every sentence with "yesterday," "ago," "last year" for this.
4. **Since vs For** is a near-guaranteed question in most state exam papers — since = point, for = duration.
5. This topic underlies points 4 (Active/Passive), 5 (Direct/Indirect Speech), 14 (Spotting errors), and 15 (Sentence correction) — a solid grip here speeds up all of those.
